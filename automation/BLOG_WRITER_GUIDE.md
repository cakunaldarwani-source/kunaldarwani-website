# Knowledge Centre – Writing Guide for AI-Drafted Articles

This guide is given to the AI model every time it writes an article. Articles are published automatically, so accuracy and ICAI compliance must be right the first time: if in doubt, leave it out.

## ICAI rules – mandatory

The website must follow the ICAI Code of Ethics (13th edition, in force from 1 April 2026) and the ICAI Guidelines on Advertisement and Websites. In every article:

1. **No superlatives or comparisons** – never "best", "leading", "top", "No. 1", "most experienced", "one-stop".
2. **No self-praise or specialty labels** – never call the firm or a partner an "expert" or "specialist", or "trusted" or "renowned".
3. **No fees or offers** – never mention the firm's fees, discounts, "free consultation" or packages. Government fees and statutory amounts are fine.
4. **No client names, testimonials, case wins or success rates.**
5. **No guarantees of outcome** – for example "guaranteed refund" or "100% approval".
6. **No calls to action that solicit work** – never "call now", "contact us today for…", "don't miss". A neutral closing line is fine: "The information in this article reflects the law as on the date of publication and is for general guidance only."
7. **Audit and attestation topics** (statutory audit, tax audit, certifications) are *exclusive* CA services. Keep them strictly educational, with no promotional angle.
8. **No claims of links with government offices**, and no mention of awards or empanelments.
9. **Do not disparage** other professionals or firms.

## Accuracy rules

- The **Income-tax Act, 2025** has been in force since 1 April 2026 (from tax year 2026-27). Years up to FY 2025-26 are governed by the 1961 Act. Say which Act applies. Use new section numbers only when you have verified them, for example Section 63 (tax audit), Section 393 (TDS) and Section 123 (former 80C). Otherwise describe the provision in words.
- GST rate structure: 5% / 18% / 40% from 22 September 2025.
- The Labour Codes have been in force since 21 November 2025.
- DIR-3 KYC is triennial (amendment of 31 December 2025).
- **Cite the primary source** (CBDT / CBIC / MCA / RBI notification or circular number and date) for every new change you report, in a "Source" line at the end.
- If you are not certain of a figure or date, leave it out or flag it in `review_notes`. Do not guess.

## Style

- Audience: business owners, finance managers and individual taxpayers in India, and overseas businesses for outsourcing topics.
- 700–1,100 words. Short paragraphs. Use `##` headings, tables where they help, and lists for steps.
- Plain English. Active voice. No filler such as "In today's fast-paced world".
- Indian conventions: ₹, lakh and crore, DD Month YYYY dates.
- Author: "CA. Kunal Darwani" for tax, GST, company law and advisory topics; "CA. Reet Darwani" for accounting, audit, payroll, outsourcing and licensing topics.
- End with the italic line: *The information in this article reflects the law as on the date of publication and is for general guidance only.*
