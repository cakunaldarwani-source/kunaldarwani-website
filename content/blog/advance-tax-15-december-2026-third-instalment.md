By **15 December 2026**, a taxpayer liable to advance tax should have paid at least **75%** of the estimated tax for tax year 2026-27, after reducing TDS and TCS. Advance tax applies when the tax payable for the year is ₹10,000 or more, and a shortfall on this date attracts interest of 3% on the unpaid amount under section 425 of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

This is the first December instalment under the new Act. The schedule has not changed, but the section numbers, the challan selection and the interest provisions now read differently, so it helps to check the position a few weeks before the date.

## Which Act applies to advance tax this year?

Income earned from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 is **tax year 2026-27**, and advance tax on it is governed by the Income-tax Act, 2025. The liability arises under section 404, the computation follows section 405 and the instalment dates are in section 408. Advance tax and interest for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) stay under the 1961 Act, including sections 234B and 234C, even where the interest is worked out during 2026-27.

The Income Tax Department's transition FAQs state that there is no change in policy on advance tax. The rules have been reorganised and renumbered. For a wider overview of the new terms, see our note on [the Income-tax Act, 2025 and the tax year](income-tax-act-2025-tax-year-regimes-itr.html).

## How much should be paid by each due date?

The cumulative amounts under section 408 are:

| Due date (tax year 2026-27) | Cumulative advance tax to be paid | Interest rate on shortfall (section 425) |
|---|---|---|
| 15 June 2026 | 15% | 3% |
| 15 September 2026 | 45% | 3% |
| 15 December 2026 | 75% | 3% |
| 15 March 2027 | 100% | 1% |

Each instalment is the cumulative percentage less what has already been paid. A taxpayer who paid 45% by September pays a further 30% by 15 December.

Two cushions apply to the first two instalments only. No interest is charged for the June instalment if at least 12% has been paid, or for the September instalment if at least 36% has been paid. There is no such cushion for 15 December: anything below 75% is a shortfall.

## Who has to pay advance tax?

Any person whose estimated tax for the year, after TDS and TCS, is **₹10,000 or more**. This covers businesses, professionals, companies, firms and LLPs, and also individuals whose salary TDS does not cover income from rent, interest, dividends, capital gains or side work.

Two groups are treated differently:

- **Resident senior citizens** (aged 60 or more during the tax year) who have no income from business or profession are not liable to pay advance tax.
- **Presumptive taxpayers** who declare income under section 58(2) (Table Sl. No. 1 or 3) pay the whole advance tax in one instalment by **15 March 2027**. They have nothing to pay on 15 December, though paying early is allowed.

TDS reduces the liability only where it has actually been deducted. If an employer or bank has not deducted tax, the tax on that income has to be covered through advance tax.

## How is interest worked out if the December instalment is short?

Two separate interest provisions apply.

**Section 425 (deferment of instalments)** looks at each due date. The shortfall is measured against the tax due on the returned income, and interest is charged at 3% for each of the first three instalments and 1% for the March instalment.

**Section 424 (default in payment)** applies if the total advance tax paid by 31 March 2027 is less than 90% of the assessed tax. Interest is 1% for every month or part of a month on the shortfall, from 1 April 2027 until the date income is determined or assessed.

A simple illustration: estimated tax after TDS is ₹4,00,000. The taxpayer pays ₹60,000 in June and ₹40,000 in September, skips December, and pays the balance of ₹3,00,000 on 15 March 2027.

| Instalment | Required (cumulative) | Paid (cumulative) | Shortfall | Interest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 June | ₹60,000 | ₹60,000 | Nil | Nil |
| 15 September | ₹1,80,000 | ₹1,00,000 | ₹80,000 | ₹2,400 |
| 15 December | ₹3,00,000 | ₹1,00,000 | ₹2,00,000 | ₹6,000 |
| 15 March | ₹4,00,000 | ₹4,00,000 | Nil | Nil |

Interest under section 425 here is ₹8,400. Section 424 does not apply, since the full tax was paid by 31 March.

## What if income was not foreseen?

Section 425(4) gives relief where the shortfall arises because capital gains, dividend income, income from a business or profession that has started for the first time, or certain other specified income could not be estimated earlier. The condition is that tax on that income is paid in the remaining instalments, or by 31 March 2027 if no instalment is left.

So a business owner who sells a property in January 2027 does not suffer interest for the December instalment on that gain, provided the tax on it goes into the 15 March payment. Section 408(3) also treats any amount paid by 31 March as advance tax for the year.

## What we commonly see in practice

A few patterns repeat every December, and some are new this year because of the change of Act:

- **Wrong year in the challan.** Advance tax for April 2026 to March 2027 must be paid against Tax Year 2026-27. Choosing AY 2026-27 by habit sends the credit to FY 2025-26, and the correction takes time while interest continues to be computed on the shortfall.
- **Capital gains forgotten until March.** Redemptions of mutual fund units and share sales during the year are often left out of the December estimate. The exemption in section 425(4) helps only if the tax is paid in the next instalment.
- **Assuming TDS covers everything.** Fixed deposit interest and rent often carry TDS at a lower rate than the taxpayer's slab, so the gap builds up quietly.
- **Partners paying only for the firm.** A firm and its partners are separate taxpayers. Partner remuneration and interest from the firm are taxable in the partner's hands and need their own estimate.
- **No working on file.** When the processing of the return shows interest under sections 424 and 425, the computation kept at the time of each payment is what helps check whether the interest is correct.

For the December estimate we usually ask for the books up to October or November, the Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS, capital gains statements, and the challans of the June and September instalments.

## How do you pay the instalment?

Advance tax is paid online through the e-Pay Tax facility on the income-tax e-filing portal, using net banking, debit card or the other modes shown there. Select Tax Year 2026-27 and the payment type "advance tax", and check the PAN on the challan before paying. A payment can be made from anyone's bank account, but the challan must carry the taxpayer's PAN. The credit usually shows in Form 26AS within a few working days.

If the Assessing Officer has issued an order asking for advance tax and your own estimate is lower, the estimate is filed electronically in Form 152 before the due date of the instalment.

Before 15 December 2026, compare the income booked up to November with the September estimate, add any capital gains already realised, and pay the difference up to 75%. The next date to plan for is 15 March 2027, when the balance becomes due and presumptive taxpayers make their single payment.
