The biggest articleship mistake a CA student makes is choosing a firm by its name rather than by the work, the mentoring and the cost of the two years. A Big Four seat suits some students. For many others, a well-run mid-sized firm gives wider exposure, closer guidance and a clearer path to both practice and a corporate job after qualifying.

## Why does this choice feel so pressured?

Right after the CA Intermediate result, thousands of students apply for the same few hundred seats in large firms in the metros. The competition is at its peak exactly when a student has the least information about what the work involves.

The numbers tell a different story about where the real competition lies. ICAI's 2025 data shows roughly 10 lakh students registered in the course, of which about 2.4 lakh are at the Final level, most of them in or through articleship. Against this, the May 2025 and September 2025 sessions together produced about 25,700 new Chartered Accountants.

| Point | Approximate figure |
|---|---|
| Students registered in the CA course (2025) | about 10 lakh |
| Students registered at the Final level (2025) | about 2.4 lakh |
| New CAs, May 2025 session | 14,247 |
| New CAs, September 2025 session | 11,466 |
| CA Final pass rate, both groups (May / Sept 2025) | 18.75% / 16.23% |

So the pool competing for articleship seats is many times larger than the pool of newly qualified CAs competing for jobs. An articleship seat is a two-year arrangement. A role after qualifying is the start of a long career, and at that stage far fewer people are in the race.

## Can you join a corporate career after qualifying?

Yes. Large firms and companies recruit qualified Chartered Accountants every year through campus placements and direct hiring. Nothing in that path requires the articleship to have been done in the same firm. A good Final result, sound practical knowledge and the ability to explain your work in an interview carry more weight at that stage.

This removes a lot of the pressure from the articleship decision. You do not have to secure your corporate career at 20. You can build your foundation first and choose your direction once you are a member.

## Big Four or mid-sized firm: how do they compare?

| Factor | Big Four and large networks | Mid-sized and smaller firms |
|---|---|---|
| Exposure | Deep work in one area or one engagement for long periods | Audit, income tax, GST, company law and accounting, often in the same year |
| Ownership of work | A defined part of a large team's file | Assignments handled from start to finish |
| Access to principal | Usually through managers and seniors | Direct and frequent |
| Location | Mostly metros | Metros, tier-2 and tier-3 cities |
| Cost to the student | Metro rent and living costs | Often living at home |

Neither column is better for every student. If you are certain you want a career in large-company audit or reporting, depth in one area has value. If you want options, including the option of your own practice, breadth matters more.

## Why breadth helps both men and women in the long run

Most CA firms in India are small. ICAI's 2025 data shows close to one lakh registered firms, and about 69% of them are proprietary firms. That is the profession most qualified CAs eventually work in or alongside.

Broad exposure during articleship keeps two doors open:

- **For women members**, a practice-ready skill set means a career that can continue in a non-metro city after marriage or relocation, either by joining a local firm or by starting a practice.
- **For men members** who begin in corporate roles, which are demanding these days, the same foundation makes it far easier to return home and start a practice with confidence, years later.

A student who has spent two years on one narrow type of engagement often has to relearn tax, GST and compliance work before starting out on their own.

## Can you afford two years in a metro?

This question is skipped more often than any other. The stipend for articleship rarely covers rent, food, travel and coaching in a metro city. In practice, most students in metros receive money from their parents every month for the full two years.

Before you commit, sit down with your family and work out:

1. Monthly rent, food and local travel in that city
2. Coaching and study material for the Final
3. The stipend you will actually receive
4. The gap, multiplied by 24 months

If that gap strains your family, a good firm in your own city may serve you better, and the money saved can support your Final preparation or your first year after qualifying.

## Common mistakes students make while choosing articleship

From years of interviewing articled assistants, these patterns come up again and again:

- **Choosing by brand alone.** The firm's name goes on your CV once. The work you do shapes how you think for decades.
- **Not knowing the domains.** Many students only know "statutory audit" and "internal audit" because those are what seniors talk about. Income tax, GST, company law, bank audit, litigation, valuation and advisory are equally available. Learn what each involves before you choose.
- **Ignoring their own strengths.** A student who enjoys law and drafting may do better in tax litigation than in audit. Every student is different.
- **Not asking about the principal.** Ask how often you will sit with the principal and who reviews your work.
- **Ignoring the cost of living.** Covered above, and often the cause of stress in the second year.
- **Following friends.** A batch moving together to one city is not a reason to go.
- **Not asking past articles.** Two conversations with students who finished articleship there tell you more than any website.

## Why your principal matters more than you think

In school, the race is often between horses and donkeys, so a good student wins easily. In CA, the race is between horses and horses, and the pass percentage is still low. At this level, the student who has someone to ask, someone who reviews their drafts and discusses their doubts, has a real advantage.

That access is easier in a firm where you sit near the principal than in one where you may meet the signing partner a few times in two years. When you are stuck on a concept before the Final, or unsure about your career, a principal who knows your work is worth a lot.

## Making the decision

Write down what you want to be doing five years after qualifying. Then pick the articleship that builds towards it, keeps your options open and fits your family's finances. For a practical checklist on visiting firms and the questions to ask, read [how to choose a CA firm for articleship](how-to-choose-ca-firm-for-articleship-agra.html). If you are in Agra, our page on [articleship at the firm](../articleship-in-agra.html) explains the work and how to apply.

Articleship decides your first two years. It does not decide your career, unless you let a brand name make that choice for you.
