For Assessment Year 2026-27 (financial year 2025-26), a **belated income tax return** can be filed up to **31 December 2026**. A **revised return** can now be filed up to **31 March 2027**, because the Finance Act, 2026 extended the revision window by three months. Revisions filed in the later part of that window attract a new fee of ₹1,000 or ₹5,000.

AY 2026-27 is still governed by the **Income-tax Act, 1961**. The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from tax year 2026-27 onwards.

## What is the last date for a belated return for AY 2026-27?

Under **section 139(4)**, a person who missed the original due date can file a belated return up to three months before the end of the assessment year, or before the assessment is completed, whichever is earlier. For AY 2026-27, that is **31 December 2026**.

The original due dates under section 139(1) for this year are:

| Taxpayer | Original due date |
|---|---|
| Transfer pricing cases (section 92E applies) | 30 November 2026 |
| Companies and other audit cases | 31 October 2026, extended to **21 November 2026** by CBDT Circular No. 07/2026 |
| Non-audit business or professional income | 31 August 2026 |
| All other taxpayers | 31 July 2026 |

For the extension in audit cases, see [CBDT extends tax audit and ITR due dates for AY 2026-27](cbdt-extends-tax-audit-itr-due-dates-ay-2026-27.html).

## What does a belated return cost?

Filing after the due date is allowed, but it has consequences:

- **Late fee under section 234F:** ₹5,000. Where total income does not exceed ₹5 lakh, the fee cannot exceed ₹1,000.
- **Interest under section 234A:** simple interest on the unpaid tax for the period of delay.
- **Losses:** losses other than loss under the head "Income from house property" cannot be carried forward from a belated return.
- **Old tax regime:** the option to stay out of the new regime under section 115BAC(6) is exercised with a return filed under section 139(1), or for business income on or before that due date. A taxpayer filing late generally cannot choose the old regime for that year.

## What changed for revised returns in 2026?

The **Finance Act, 2026** (Act No. 4 of 2026, assented on 30 March 2026) substituted **section 139(5)** with effect from 1 March 2026. The new provision says that a person who has filed a return under section 139(1) **or section 139(4)** and finds an omission or wrong statement may file a revised return **before the end of the relevant assessment year** or before completion of the assessment, whichever is earlier.

For AY 2026-27, the end of the assessment year is **31 March 2027**. Earlier, the limit was three months before the end of the assessment year, that is, 31 December.

## When does the new section 234-I fee apply?

The same Act inserted **section 234-I**. A fee is payable on a revised return filed in the later part of the window:

| Total income | Fee under section 234-I |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹5 lakh | ₹1,000 |
| Above ₹5 lakh | ₹5,000 |

The Memorandum explaining the Finance Bill, 2026 describes this fee as applying to revised returns filed **beyond nine months from the end of the relevant tax year**. For AY 2026-27, that means revisions filed after 31 December 2026. A revision filed on or before 31 December 2026 does not attract this fee.

## Belated, revised or updated return: which one applies?

| Situation | Return to file | Time limit for AY 2026-27 |
|---|---|---|
| Original due date missed | Belated return, section 139(4) | 31 December 2026 |
| Mistake or omission in a filed return | Revised return, section 139(5) | 31 March 2027 (fee after 31 December 2026) |
| Income missed and the above windows have closed | Updated return (ITR-U), section 139(8A) | 48 months from the end of the assessment year |

An updated return cannot be used to report a loss, reduce the tax liability or increase a refund. It also requires **additional tax of 25% to 70%** of the tax and interest payable, depending on how late it is filed.

## What should you check before filing or revising?

1. **Match AIS and Form 26AS.** Annual Information Statement entries often update after the original filing. Compare interest, dividends, securities transactions and TDS credits before revising.
2. **Pay tax and interest first.** Self-assessment tax and interest under sections 234A, 234B and 234C should be paid before the return is uploaded.
3. **Do not leave revisions to the last week.** A revision is possible only until the assessment is completed. If a notice has been issued, take advice on whether revision is still open.
4. **Keep the acknowledgement numbers.** A revised return quotes the acknowledgement number and date of the return being revised.
5. **Plan for tax year 2026-27.** Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, the Memorandum states that revised returns can similarly be filed up to twelve months from the end of the tax year, with a fee under section 428(b) for revisions beyond nine months.
