Many companies and LLPs are formed for a project that never takes off, or a business that later moves into another entity. Leaving them dormant is not free: annual filings continue, and missed filings lead to penalties and director disqualification. Formal closure is usually the better choice.

## Route 1: Strike off (fast track exit)

**For companies – Section 248 and STK-2.** A company can apply to have its name removed from the register if:

- it has **not commenced business** within one year of incorporation, **or**
- it has **not carried on any business or operation for the two immediately preceding financial years** and has not applied for dormant status.

The company must have **no assets and no liabilities** on the date of application, or its liabilities must be fully provided for. It also must not be:

- listed on a stock exchange
- under investigation or inspection, or facing prosecution
- subject to pending litigation, or an application for compromise or arrangement
- a Section 8 company that has not obtained clearance

A government fee of ₹10,000 applies. **All pending annual filings, AOC-4 and MGT-7, must be completed up to the end of the financial year in which the company ceased business** before applying.

**For LLPs – Form 24.** An LLP that has not carried on business for one year or more can apply for strike off. Pending Form 8 and Form 11 filings must be completed first.

**C-PACE.** Since 2023, strike-off applications are processed centrally by the **Centre for Processing Accelerated Corporate Exit** at IICA, Manesar. This has substantially reduced processing time compared with the earlier jurisdictional ROC process.

## Documents for a company strike off

- a **statement of accounts** showing assets and liabilities, made up to a date not more than 30 days before the application and certified by a Chartered Accountant
- **indemnity bond** by each director (STK-3)
- **affidavit** by each director (STK-4)
- **special resolution**, or consent of 75% of the members by paid-up capital
- the latest **income tax return** acknowledgement
- a statement about pending litigation, if any

## What to close before applying

1. **Collect receivables and settle payables.** The balance sheet must be clean.
2. **Close bank accounts**, and get a closure certificate from the bank.
3. **Cancel GST registration** (REG-16) and file the final return GSTR-10.
4. **Surrender TAN** after filing the final TDS statements.
5. **Close PF and ESI registrations**, and cancel the IEC, FSSAI and other licences.
6. **File the final income tax return.**

Directors remain liable for liabilities that come to light later. The indemnity they sign is enforceable, and the Registrar can restore a struck-off company on application by an aggrieved person within 20 years.

## Route 2: Voluntary liquidation

Where the company has **assets to distribute** or **liabilities to settle**, the strike-off route is not available, or not advisable. **Voluntary liquidation** under Section 59 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code involves:

- a declaration of solvency by the majority of directors, with an audited statement of affairs and a valuation report
- a special resolution of the members, and approval of creditors holding two-thirds of the debt where there is debt
- appointment of an **insolvency professional** as liquidator, who realises the assets, pays the creditors and distributes the surplus
- a final order from the NCLT dissolving the company

It takes longer and costs more than strike off, but gives a clean, court-approved closure.

## Which route to choose

- Company never started business, or is fully wound down with nothing left → **strike off**
- Company has property, investments or creditors to deal with → **voluntary liquidation**
- Company is temporarily inactive and may restart → consider **dormant status** under Section 455
