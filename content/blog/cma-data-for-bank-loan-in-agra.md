CMA data for a bank loan in Agra is the same standard set of financial statements that every bank in India asks for: past results, the current year's estimate and projections, arranged so the credit officer can work out how much working capital or term loan the business can support. What changes locally is the business mix. Footwear units, marble and stone handicraft workshops, petha makers, hotels and traders around the city each have their own stock cycles, and the CMA has to show that cycle honestly.

This article covers the format, the numbers banks focus on and the practical issues we see when Agra businesses apply for a new limit or a renewal. For the overall structure of a project report, see our earlier article on [preparing a project report and CMA data](project-report-cma-data-bank-loan.html).

## What does a CMA report for a bank loan contain?

Most banks use a six-form layout, sometimes with their own cover sheets added.

| Form | What it shows | What the banker checks |
|---|---|---|
| I. Existing and proposed limits | Present facilities with all banks and the limits now requested | Total exposure and multiple banking |
| II. Operating statement | Sales, cost of sales, interest, profit and tax, year by year | Growth rate and margin trend |
| III. Analysis of balance sheet | Liabilities and assets regrouped into current, term and net worth | Net worth, TOL/TNW, current ratio |
| IV. Current assets and liabilities | Holding levels of raw material, stock, debtors and creditors in months or days | Whether stock and debtor periods are realistic |
| V. Computation of bank finance | Working capital gap, borrower's margin and permissible bank finance | The limit that can be sanctioned |
| VI. Fund flow | Long-term and short-term sources and uses of funds | Whether term funds are being diverted |

The columns usually run from two audited years, through the current year's estimate, to projections. A term loan needs projections for the entire repayment period.

## How do banks work out the working capital limit?

For micro and small enterprises needing fund-based working capital up to ₹5 crore, RBI's long-standing guidance (from the Nayak Committee) is the turnover method. The requirement is taken at 25% of projected annual turnover. The borrower brings 5% of turnover as margin, and the bank finances at least 20%.

A quick example: if projected sales are ₹2 crore, the working capital need is ₹50 lakh, the borrower's margin is ₹10 lakh and bank finance can be ₹40 lakh. Where the traditional holding-period method gives a higher figure, the bank may sanction that higher amount. Above ₹5 crore, banks use their own methods, commonly the holding-period (MPBF style) approach or a cash budget.

This is why Form IV matters. An Agra footwear unit that stocks up before the winter and wedding seasons, or a marble workshop holding semi-finished pieces for months, must show those holding periods with reasons. Average figures that ignore the peak leave the business short of funds exactly when it needs them.

## Which ratios decide the outcome?

| Ratio | Usual comfort level |
|---|---|
| DSCR (for term loans) | Average around 1.5, and not below about 1.2 in any year |
| Current ratio | Around 1.33 for working capital, though banks now set their own floor |
| TOL/TNW | Generally below 3 to 4, depending on the sector |
| Interest coverage | Comfortably above 2 |
| Stock and debtor holding | In line with past years and the trade |

These are typical internal benchmarks, not legal limits. Each bank's credit policy decides the final threshold.

## Recent RBI changes that help small borrowers

- **Collateral-free limit raised:** banks must not take collateral security for loans up to ₹20 lakh to micro and small enterprises. This applies to loans sanctioned or renewed on or after 1 April 2026 (earlier ₹10 lakh). Banks may go up to ₹25 lakh without collateral based on track record.
- **CGTMSE cover:** the credit guarantee ceiling for MSE loans was raised to ₹10 crore from 1 April 2025, so many collateral-light loans can carry guarantee cover.
- **Decision timeline:** for MSE loans up to ₹25 lakh, the credit decision should not take more than 14 working days.
- **Composite loan:** banks can sanction a composite limit of up to ₹1 crore covering both working capital and term loan through a single window.

Udyam registration is the starting point for most of these benefits, so check it is current before applying.

## Documents to keep ready

- Audited financial statements and ITRs for the last two or three years
- GST returns (GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B) for the same period, plus the current year to date
- Udyam registration certificate and KYC of the business and promoters
- Stock and debtor statements as on a recent date
- Sanction letters and account statements of existing loans
- For term loans: quotations for machinery, building estimates and, where relevant, land or lease papers
- Projections with the assumptions behind sales growth, margins and holding periods

## What we commonly see in practice

The most frequent query from credit officers is a turnover mismatch. The CMA shows one sales figure, the GST returns another and the ITR a third. Usually there is a valid reason, such as stock transfers or branch billing, but it has to be reconciled on paper before the file goes to the bank.

Other patterns we see:

- Projections showing sales jumping 40% or 50% without new capacity, orders or premises to support them.
- Stock holding in Form IV that does not match the stock statements the business already submits monthly for its existing limit.
- Promoter contribution shown as "own funds" without bank statements, or brought in as unsecured loans that the bank later asks to be subordinated.
- Machinery quotations that are months old or from suppliers who cannot be traced.
- Seasonal businesses (hotels, handicraft exporters, footwear makers) applying with year-average figures, then running out of limit at peak season.

A CMA that reconciles with the returns, explains every jump and shows the real seasonal cycle generally moves through credit appraisal with fewer rounds of queries. Our [project finance](../services/project-finance.html) page sets out how we work on these files.

## Before you apply

If your existing limit comes up for renewal in the next quarter, start with the reconciliation: match the books, GST returns and ITR for the last two years, and note the reasons for any difference. Then build the projections from that base, with stock and debtor periods for your busiest months. Units planning an expansion this financial year should also check whether the new loan falls within the ₹20 lakh collateral-free limit or can be covered under CGTMSE, since that changes the security the bank will ask for.
