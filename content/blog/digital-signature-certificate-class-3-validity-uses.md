A Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) is a secure digital key that verifies the identity of the signer. Under the Information Technology Act, 2000, a document signed with a valid DSC has the same legal recognition as a document signed by hand.

## Types of DSC

Since **1 January 2021**, the Controller of Certifying Authorities has discontinued Class 2 certificates. **Class 3** is now the standard for individuals and organisations.

| Type | Use |
|---|---|
| **Class 3 – Signing** | MCA, income tax, GST, EPFO and general document signing |
| **Class 3 – Signing + Encryption** | E-tenders and e-procurement portals, where bids are encrypted |
| **DGFT DSC** | IEC and other DGFT applications |
| **Organisation DSC** | Issued to an individual on behalf of an organisation, with the organisation's name in the certificate |

A DSC is usually issued for **one, two or three years**.

## How a DSC is issued

DSCs are issued by **Certifying Authorities** licensed by the CCA. The process involves:

1. an online application with ID and address proof. **Aadhaar e-KYC** or **PAN-based** verification is common
2. **video verification**, in which the applicant reads a displayed code on camera
3. mobile and email OTP verification
4. download of the certificate onto a **FIPS-compliant USB token**, which is required for most portals

The private key is generated and stored on the token, and only the holder should control it. **Never share your token and PIN.** Anything signed with your DSC is legally attributed to you.

## Documents usually asked for

For an **individual DSC**:

- PAN card
- Aadhaar, or another identity and address proof accepted by the Certifying Authority
- a recent photograph, and access to the mobile number and email to be recorded in the certificate

For an **organisation DSC**, in addition:

- the organisation's PAN, and its incorporation certificate, partnership deed or GST registration
- an authorisation letter or board resolution naming the applicant as the authorised signatory
- the applicant's identity proof and the organisation's address proof

Foreign nationals and NRIs follow a separate verification process, usually with passport copies and attested documents.

## Where you need a DSC

- **MCA** – all e-forms are signed by directors, and certified by professionals where required
- **Income tax** – returns of companies, and of taxpayers whose accounts are audited, must be verified with DSC. Others may use Aadhaar OTP or EVC
- **GST** – companies and LLPs must sign returns with DSC. Others may use EVC
- **DGFT** – IEC and other applications
- **E-tenders** – government procurement portals, including GeM-linked processes
- **EPFO** – employer-side approvals

## Registering a DSC on each portal

Getting the certificate is only half the job. Each portal must link the certificate to your user ID before you can sign:

- **MCA V3:** log in and associate the DSC with your user ID from the DSC menu. The name and PAN in the certificate must match the director's or professional's records.
- **GST:** register the DSC under the authorised signatory's profile. The emSigner utility must be installed and running on the computer.
- **Income tax e-filing:** register the DSC from the profile section, using the token and the signing utility provided on the portal.

The portals read the token through browser-based utilities, so keep the token driver and the signing utility updated after any computer change.

## What we commonly see in practice

Most DSC problems come down to three things. The certificate expires in the week of a deadline because nobody tracked the date. The name on the DSC is spelt differently from the PAN or the MCA records, so the portal refuses the association. Or the token sits with a former employee or an outside agent, and the director does not know where it is. A register with expiry dates and custody details, reviewed each quarter, avoids almost all of these. ## Managing DSCs in a business

- Keep a **register of DSCs** with the holder, type, issuing authority and expiry date.
- **Renew** before expiry. An expired DSC cannot be used, and filings near deadlines get stuck.
- **Re-register** a renewed DSC on each portal. MCA, GST and income tax each need the new certificate to be registered.
- When a director or signatory leaves, **revoke** their DSC registration from the company's portals.
- Store tokens securely and record who has custody of them.
- Check expiry dates before 30 June and 30 September each year, when DPT-3, DIR-3 KYC (in the years it falls due) and the annual filings bunch together.
