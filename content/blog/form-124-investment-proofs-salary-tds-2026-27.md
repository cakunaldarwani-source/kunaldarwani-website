**Form No. 124** is the statement a salaried employee gives the employer to claim house rent allowance, leave travel concession, home loan interest and investment deductions while salary TDS is being worked out. It replaces Form 12BB from tax year 2026-27 and is prescribed by Rule 205 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, read with section 392(5)(b) of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Most employers start collecting these proofs between December and February, so this is the time to get the paperwork in order.

## What changed from Form 12BB?

The purpose is the same. What has changed is the legal reference and some of the wording.

| Point | Up to FY 2025-26 | From tax year 2026-27 |
|---|---|---|
| Law | Section 192, Income-tax Act, 1961 | Section 392, Income-tax Act, 2025 |
| Rule | Rule 26C, Income-tax Rules, 1962 | Rule 205, Income-tax Rules, 2026 |
| Form | Form 12BB | Form No. 124 |
| Year referred to | Financial year / previous year | Tax year |
| Salary TDS certificate issued later | Form 16 | Form No. 130 |

The form now has two parts. **Part A** carries the employee's name, address, PAN, contact details and the tax year. **Part B** lists the claims and the evidence for each, with further details attached as an annexure. It ends with a declaration by the employee that the particulars are correct and complete.

## What evidence does Form 124 ask for?

Rule 205(2) sets out the evidence for each type of claim. The department's FAQ on Form 124 then lists the supporting documents that usually go with it.

| Claim | Particulars required by Rule 205 | Supporting documents (department FAQ) |
|---|---|---|
| House rent allowance | Name, address and PAN of the landlord where aggregate rent for the tax year exceeds ₹1,00,000, and relationship with the landlord, if any | Copy of rent agreement |
| Leave travel concession or assistance | Evidence of expenditure | Documents supporting the LTC claim |
| Interest on housing loan (income from house property) | Name, address and PAN of the lender | Copy of loan agreement |
| Investment and expenditure deductions | Evidence of investment or expenditure | Documents supporting the investment or expenditure |

Two points stand out. The relationship with the landlord is now a specific field, so rent paid to a parent or spouse has to be disclosed as such. And the lender's PAN is required for every home loan interest claim, with no monetary threshold.

## Do employees in the new tax regime need Form 124?

The new regime is the default for salary TDS. Under it, the HRA exemption, leave travel concession, interest on a self-occupied house and most investment deductions (such as the ₹1.5 lakh deduction formerly under Section 80C, now Section 123) are not available. An employee who stays in the new regime therefore usually has little or nothing to claim through Form 124. The standard deduction of ₹75,000 is allowed without any form.

The employer's contribution to NPS remains deductible under the new regime, but that is processed by the employer from its own payroll records.

An employee who has told the employer that the old regime should be used for TDS must back that choice with proofs. If the claims are not supported, the employer computes TDS without them. The department's regime FAQ also makes clear that telling the employer is not the same as opting out in the return: the final choice is made when the return is filed, so a different regime can still be picked at that stage if it works out better.

## How and when should Form 124 be submitted?

- **To whom:** the current employer, not the income tax portal.
- **How:** electronically or on paper, as the employer's process allows.
- **How often:** once for the tax year. The department's note says it should be submitted as early as possible so that tax can be spread over the remaining months.
- **Job change:** details of salary and TDS from the previous employer go to the new employer in **Form No. 122**.

Employers set their own internal cut-off, usually in January or February, so that the final adjustment can be made in the last salary runs before 31 March 2027.

## What we commonly see in practice

Proof season produces the same problems every year, on both the employee and the employer side.

- **Landlord PAN missing.** Annual rent crosses ₹1,00,000 at about ₹8,334 a month, which covers most urban rentals. Employees often submit receipts without the landlord's PAN, and the HRA claim then gets held back.
- **Rent paid to family members without an agreement or bank trail.** Cash rent receipts signed by a parent are usually the first thing an assessing officer questions later, typically by asking for the rent agreement, bank statements and whether the parent has shown the rent as income.
- **Home loan certificates that show only total EMI.** The claim needs the interest and principal split for the tax year, plus the lender's PAN. Co-borrowers sometimes both claim the full interest.
- **Declarations that never turn into proofs.** An employee declares investments in April, TDS is kept low all year, and the proofs never arrive. The shortfall is then recovered from the January to March salaries, which can sharply cut take-home pay.
- **Previous employer income left out.** Someone who changed jobs mid-year does not file Form No. 122. Each employer gives the slab benefit separately, and a tax demand surfaces when the return is filed.
- **Claims that do not match the return.** Deductions shown in the return that differ from those in the salary certificate are a frequent trigger for department communications. Officers usually ask for the same documents that should have gone with Form 124.

On the employer side, the common gap is accepting a declaration as if it were evidence. Rule 205 speaks of evidence or particulars of claims, so payroll teams keep the supporting documents on file in case the TDS computation is examined. Where these checks are run through an outsourced [payroll service](../services/payroll.html), the document list and cut-off date should be fixed in writing at the start of the year.

## A short checklist for employees

1. Confirm with your employer which regime is being used for your TDS.
2. If the old regime applies, collect the rent agreement, receipts and landlord PAN, the loan interest certificate with lender PAN, LTC bills and investment receipts.
3. Disclose any relationship with the landlord.
4. If you changed jobs this tax year, submit Form No. 122 with the previous employer's salary and TDS figures.
5. Keep copies of everything you submit; they will be needed again when you file your return.

The date to watch is your employer's internal proof cut-off, usually in January or February 2027. After that, any shortfall is adjusted in the last salary runs before 31 March 2027, and the salary certificate in Form No. 130 is due by 15 June 2027. Proofs submitted on time keep the March salary intact and make the return easier to reconcile.
