The Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 requires every food business, from a tea stall to a manufacturing plant, to be registered or licensed with FSSAI. Applying in the wrong category is common. It leads to rejection, or to a licence that does not legally cover what you sell.

## The three categories

| Category | Who it is for (broadly) |
|---|---|
| **Basic Registration** | Petty food businesses with annual turnover up to ₹12 lakh, such as small retailers, hawkers, home-based sellers and temporary stalls |
| **State Licence** | Turnover above ₹12 lakh and up to ₹20 crore, and mid-sized manufacturers, storage units, restaurants and caterers within the capacity limits |
| **Central Licence** | Turnover above ₹20 crore, and certain activities regardless of turnover |

For manufacturers, the category also depends on **production capacity**, for example litres of milk per day or tonnes per day of other food. A small dairy can need a State licence even when its turnover is low.

## Activities that always need a Central licence

- **Importers** of food items, including food ingredients and additives
- **E-commerce food business operators**, meaning the platform operator. Sellers on the platform need a licence or registration for their own premises
- **100% export-oriented units**
- Food businesses operating in **premises of Central Government agencies**, such as railways, airports and seaports
- Operators with premises in **more than one state**, which need a Central licence for the head office in addition to State licences for each unit

## E-commerce and cloud kitchens

If you sell food online, through your own website or through aggregators:

- each kitchen or warehouse needs its own registration or licence
- the licence number must appear on the platform listing and on invoices
- the platform will verify your licence and can delist you if it lapses
- labelling and display rules apply to online listings as well as to packages

For businesses selling on ONDC or marketplace platforms, the platform's own onboarding checks now include FSSAI validation, so keep the licence details consistent with your GST and trade name.

## Validity, renewal and annual returns

- A licence or registration can be taken for **one to five years**. Renew it before expiry. Late renewal attracts a penalty for each day of delay, and after a point a fresh application is needed.
- **Manufacturers and importers holding a licence** must file an **annual return in Form D-1** by **31 May** for the previous financial year. A late fee applies for each day of delay. Many licensees overlook this until they apply for renewal.
- Any change in products, capacity, premises or partners requires a **modification** application before the change is made.

## How to apply on FoSCoS

1. On the FoSCoS portal, choose **Apply for License/Registration** and select the state where the premises are located.
2. Pick the kind of business carefully (manufacturer, storage, retail, catering and so on). For manufacturers, the eligibility depends on production capacity.
3. Fill in **Form A** for basic registration or **Form B** for a State or Central licence, with GST, PAN or CIN where applicable.
4. Select the food categories and products you will deal in.
5. Upload the documents and pay the fee online.
6. Note the 17-digit reference number and track the application. Respond to any query from the authority within the time given, or the application may be rejected.

## Documents usually asked for

The portal asks for different documents by category and kind of business. The ones that come up most often are:

- photo identity and address proof of the proprietor, partners or directors
- proof of possession of the premises (sale deed, rent agreement or NOC from the owner)
- layout plan of the processing area, for manufacturers
- list of equipment and machinery, with capacity
- water analysis report from a recognised laboratory, where water is used as an ingredient
- a food safety management system plan
- authority letter naming the person responsible, and partnership deed or incorporation documents

## What we commonly see in practice

The most common errors are choosing basic registration when turnover or capacity has already crossed the limit, and listing too few food categories so that the licence does not cover all the products sold. Renewal is often left to the final week, and the Form D-1 annual return is discovered only at that point. Businesses that move premises sometimes continue on the old licence instead of applying for modification. ## Practical tips

- Select **food categories** carefully on FoSCoS. Selling products outside the categories in your licence is a violation.
- Keep a **water test report** and pest control records ready if you are a manufacturer.
- A **Food Safety Supervisor** trained under FoSTaC is required for licensed premises.
- Diary **31 May** each year for Form D-1 if you are a licensed manufacturer or importer, and the expiry date of the licence so that renewal is filed well before it lapses.
