The GST composition scheme lets a small registered business pay tax at a flat rate on its turnover instead of charging GST on every sale. Under Section 10(1) of the CGST Act, manufacturers, traders and restaurants with an aggregate turnover of up to ₹1.5 crore in the previous year pay 1% or 5%; under Section 10(2A), service providers with turnover up to ₹50 lakh pay 6%. In exchange, the business cannot collect tax from customers or claim input tax credit.

The GST portal has changed the way the scheme is selected. A GSTN advisory dated 1 November 2026 split the composition categories into two mutually exclusive options and added a facility to change the category after registration. The revised category list also appears in Forms CMP-02 and CMP-04.

## Who can opt for the composition scheme?

There are two routes, and a business uses only one of them.

| | Section 10(1) | Section 10(2A) |
|---|---|---|
| Meant for | Manufacturers, traders, restaurants | Service providers, and mixed suppliers not eligible under 10(1) |
| Turnover limit (preceding year) | ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh in Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim, Tripura and Uttarakhand) | ₹50 lakh |
| Services allowed | Up to 10% of the previous year's turnover in the State or ₹5 lakh, whichever is higher | No separate cap within the ₹50 lakh |
| Rate (CGST + SGST) | 1% manufacturers, 1% traders, 5% restaurants | 6% |

The rate is charged differently for each type of supplier:

- **Manufacturers** pay 1% on their turnover in the State.
- **Traders** pay 1% only on the turnover of taxable supplies. Exempt sales, such as fresh vegetables sold by a kirana store, are left out of the base.
- **Restaurants** (supply of food as a service under paragraph 6(b) of Schedule II) pay 5% on turnover.
- **Section 10(2A) suppliers** pay 6% on turnover in the State.

Uttar Pradesh, including Agra, follows the ₹1.5 crore limit. The test is on aggregate turnover, which covers every GSTIN under the same PAN, and all those GSTINs must be in composition together.

## Who is not allowed to opt?

The scheme is closed to:

- businesses making any inter-State outward supply of goods or services;
- suppliers of goods or services not leviable to GST;
- anyone supplying services through an e-commerce operator that collects tax at source under Section 52;
- manufacturers of notified goods: ice cream and other edible ice, pan masala, and tobacco and its substitutes;
- casual taxable persons and non-resident taxable persons.

Since 1 October 2023, a composition dealer may sell goods (not services) within the State through an online marketplace.

## What changed on the portal in November 2026?

GSTN's advisory of 1 November 2026 applies to applications in Forms REG-01, CMP-02, CMP-04 and REG-14:

1. **Option A** covers the Section 10(1) categories: manufacturers (other than of notified goods), restaurant suppliers under paragraph 6(b) of Schedule II, and other suppliers eligible under Section 10(1). More than one of these can be ticked.
2. **Option B** is a new single category: "Person eligible for composition levy under Section 10(2A)".
3. Option A and Option B cannot be selected together.
4. A composition taxpayer can now **change or update the category through REG-14**, and must keep the category consistent across all GSTINs linked to the same PAN.

Ticking a category on the portal does not by itself make a business eligible for that rate. Section 10(2A) is open only to a person who cannot opt under Section 10(1), so a trader with incidental services within the 10% or ₹5 lakh allowance belongs in Option A at 1%, not Option B at 6%.

## Which forms and due dates apply to a composition taxpayer?

| Form | Purpose | Due date |
|---|---|---|
| REG-01 | Opt in while applying for a new registration | With the application |
| CMP-02 | Existing taxpayer opting in | Before the financial year begins (by 31 March 2027 for FY 2027-28) |
| ITC-03 | Reverse credit on stock held when opting in | Within 60 days from the start of the financial year |
| CMP-08 | Quarterly statement and tax payment | 18th of the month after the quarter (18 January 2027 for October to December 2026) |
| GSTR-4 | Annual return | 30 June after the financial year (30 June 2027 for FY 2026-27) |
| CMP-04 | Withdrawal from the scheme | Within 7 days of becoming ineligible, or before the date of a voluntary exit |
| ITC-01 | Claim credit on stock after leaving | Within 30 days of withdrawal |

The tax in CMP-08 is paid from the electronic cash ledger. Every bill of supply must carry the words "composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies" at the top, and every place of business must display "composition taxable person" on its signboard.

## What happens when turnover crosses the limit?

The option lapses from the day the aggregate turnover during the year exceeds the limit, not from the next financial year. From that day the business must issue tax invoices and file regular returns. CMP-04 is due within 7 days, and ITC-01 can then be filed within 30 days to claim credit on inputs and goods held in stock on that date.

## Where composition dealers usually go wrong

Most of the problems we see in composition cases come from a few repeated patterns:

- **One stray inter-State sale.** A single courier order to a buyer in another State, or an online booking delivered across the border, makes the business ineligible. Officers usually pick this up from e-way bills.
- **Turnover tracked only per GSTIN.** Owners with a second registration under the same PAN, or a shop and a small service unit, check the limit for one GSTIN and cross the PAN-level limit without noticing.
- **GST shown on the bill.** Billing software set up for a regular dealer keeps adding a tax line. Any amount collected as tax has to be deposited with the government, over and above the composition tax.
- **Wrong category.** A service provider files under Option A and pays 1%, or a trader picks Option B and pays 6%. With the new REG-14 facility, the category can be corrected, but the past tax difference still has to be worked out.
- **CMP-08 filed, GSTR-4 forgotten.** The quarterly payment is made, but the annual return is not, and late fee builds up. Officers then ask for the purchase register, because GSTR-4 reports inward supplies invoice-wise.
- **Reverse charge ignored.** Composition dealers still pay tax on inward supplies under reverse charge, in cash, and this is often missed in the quarterly statement.

Documents usually asked for in a scrutiny are the sales and purchase registers, the bill of supply series, e-way bills, bank statements and a reconciliation of CMP-08 turnover with the books.

For the registration side of the decision, see [GST registration thresholds and compulsory cases](gst-registration-thresholds-compulsory-cases-rejections.html). Our page on [GST returns](../services/gst-returns.html) explains how quarterly and annual filings are handled.

## What to check before 18 January 2027

Before the CMP-08 for October to December 2026 is filed, log in and confirm that the category shown on the portal matches the actual business (Option A or Option B) for every GSTIN under the PAN, and correct it through REG-14 if needed. Businesses thinking of entering the scheme for FY 2027-28 should test the FY 2026-27 turnover and the share of registered buyers who need input tax credit, because the CMP-02 window closes on 31 March 2027.
