Changing your GST registration details is now quicker for most fields. From 1 November 2026, the substituted Rule 19 of the CGST Rules, 2017 makes a REG-14 amendment take effect as soon as the application is submitted and the online validation is completed, unless the change concerns the principal place of business, which still goes to the proper officer. The application must be filed within fifteen days of the change, and the OTP checks must be finished within three days of submission.

The new rule was inserted by Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026 (the Central Goods and Services Tax (Amendment) Rules, 2026), which also substituted FORM GST REG-14 itself. GSTN published an advisory on the revised portal flow on 1 November 2026.

## Which GST amendments no longer need officer approval?

Under the old procedure, the portal separated "core" fields (legal name, principal and additional places of business, and partners or directors) from "non-core" fields. Core changes waited for the officer. The substituted Rule 19 narrows the officer's role to a short list.

| Change | Rule | How it takes effect |
|---|---|---|
| Principal place of business (regular registration) | 19(2) | Officer approval in REG-15, or deemed approval |
| Principal place of business, Rule 14A registrant | 19(3) | On submission and online validation, from 1 December 2026 |
| Legal name (PAN unchanged) | 19(4) | On submission and online validation |
| Addition, deletion or retirement of partners, directors, Karta, trustees, CEO | 19(4) | On submission and online validation |
| Additional places of business, goods and services, bank account, authorised signatory | 19(4) | On submission and online validation |
| Mobile number or e-mail of authorised signatory | 19(6) | Only after online verification on the portal |
| Constitution change that changes the PAN | 19(7) | Not an amendment: fresh registration needed |

Two points are easy to miss. First, a change in legal name or in partners, directors or similar persons now applies to every registration held under the same PAN in any State, so a business with several GSTINs files once and the change carries across. Second, the place of business of a representative, the entity details for a UIN holder and a GST Practitioner's professional address also remain with the officer under Rule 19(2).

## How long does the officer take to approve a change in principal place of business?

For a regular registration, the timeline in Rule 19(2) is:

| Step | Time limit |
|---|---|
| Officer approves a complete application (REG-15) | 15 days from submission |
| Officer issues a query in REG-03 | 15 days from submission |
| Applicant replies in REG-04 | 7 working days from receiving the notice |
| Officer approves after a satisfactory reply | 7 working days from receiving the reply |

If the officer does not act within these periods, the certificate is deemed to be amended and the revised certificate is made available on the portal. Where the officer is not satisfied with the reply, the application can be rejected in REG-05 with reasons recorded in writing. If no reply is filed within the seven working days, the application is deemed rejected and this is communicated in the new FORM REG-05A.

A change approved by the officer takes effect from the date of approval, not from the date of the application.

## What is the 3-day OTP rule in the new REG-14?

The revised form makes online verification of the mobile number and e-mail of the Primary Authorised Signatory and one selected promoter, partner, director or equivalent mandatory for every amendment application. Instruction 6 of REG-14 gives three days from the date of submission to complete this. If the verification is not completed, the Application Reference Number (ARN) is not generated.

In practice this means the person selected for OTP must be reachable on the mobile number and e-mail recorded on the portal. If that person has left the business, or the number is no longer in use, the contact details need to be corrected first, because a mobile or e-mail change is itself carried out only after online verification.

## How do you file a REG-14 amendment on the GST portal?

1. Log in at www.gst.gov.in and go to Services, then Registration, then Amendment of Registration (core or non-core, as the portal shows for the field).
2. Edit only the fields that changed, and upload the documents that would have been required for that particular in the original application.
3. For a new principal place of business, upload proof of possession: ownership document, rent or lease agreement, or consent letter with the owner's proof, and check that the address, PIN code and map location match.
4. Verify the application with DSC, e-signature or EVC.
5. Complete the OTP verification of the selected persons within three days and note the ARN.
6. Download the amended certificate once it is issued, or track the REG-03 query if the change went to the officer.

The declaration in the form states that the change does not warrant cancellation of the registration, so a business that has actually closed down at the old address and not restarted elsewhere should look at cancellation rather than amendment. ## Common mistakes with GST registration amendments

These are the patterns we see most often when an amendment stalls:

- **Filing months after the change.** A shop moves or a partner retires, and the registration is updated only when a notice or an e-way bill problem arises. Rule 19(1) allows fifteen days.
- **OTP sent to someone who has left.** The selected director or partner no longer uses the registered number, the three days run out and no ARN is generated.
- **Rent agreement and bill in different names.** For a new principal place of business, officers usually ask for a signed rent agreement, a recent electricity bill or property tax receipt, and a consent letter where the premises belong to a relative. A mismatch between these documents is the most common reason for a REG-03 query.
- **Trying to amend a PAN change.** A proprietorship that becomes a partnership firm or company has a new PAN and needs a fresh registration. REG-14 cannot carry it.
- **Address changed during suspension.** The revised form does not permit a change in principal place of business details while the registration is suspended or cancelled.
- **Forgetting the knock-on updates.** After a name or address change, the e-invoice and e-way bill masters, bank records and invoice stationery still show the old details for weeks.

## What changes on 1 December 2026?

Rule 19(3) comes into force on 1 December 2026. From that date, a taxpayer registered under the simplified Rule 14A route who has not opted to withdraw from it can change the principal place of business without officer approval: the certificate stands amended on submission and online validation. Instruction 7 of REG-14 is revised from the same date to exclude these taxpayers from the officer-approval requirement.

If your registration was granted under Rule 14A and you have a shift of premises planned for December, filing after 1 December 2026 lets the new address apply on submission; a regular registrant should keep the rent agreement, a recent utility bill and the owner's consent ready before filing so that the fifteen-day officer window is not lost to a query.
