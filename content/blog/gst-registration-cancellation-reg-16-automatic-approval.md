A GST registration can be cancelled on your own request by filing FORM GST REG-16 on the GST portal within 30 days of the event that ends the need for it, such as closure or transfer of the business. Since **1 November 2026**, the system approves most of these applications without an officer, provided all returns are filed and, where the business had B2B output tax above ₹2.5 lakh in any month, the final return GSTR-10 is filed within three days.

The change comes from the substituted Rule 20 of the CGST Rules, 2017, inserted by Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026, following the recommendations of the 57th GST Council meeting in October 2026. GSTN issued an advisory on the revised portal flow on 1 November 2026.

## When can you apply to cancel GST registration?

Section 29(1) of the CGST Act allows a registered person to seek cancellation when:

- the business is discontinued, or fully transferred (including on amalgamation, demerger or the death of the proprietor);
- the constitution of the business changes; or
- the person is no longer liable to be registered, for example where turnover has stayed below the threshold and registration is no longer needed.

The application must be filed within **30 days** of the event. This time limit does not apply where the sole proprietor has died; the legal heir applies instead. REG-16 asks for the stock held and the liabilities as on the day before the application or the requested cancellation date, along with the tax payable on that stock.

A change of constitution that also changes the PAN (a proprietorship converting into a company, say) needs a fresh registration for the new entity and cancellation of the old one. Where the PAN does not change, an amendment in REG-14 is usually enough.

## What must be filed before REG-16 is accepted?

The portal now checks returns before it lets the application through:

| Check | What the rule requires |
|---|---|
| Returns up to the cancellation date | Every return for tax periods ending on or before the requested date, including CMP-08 for composition taxpayers |
| GSTR-1 or IFF filed for a period | GSTR-3B for that same period must also be filed |
| Returns falling due after the application | Must be filed by their due dates; the application waits in "Pending for Returns Filing" until then |
| Dues | Tax, interest and other amounts must be paid |

If a return that falls due after the application is filed late, the case leaves the automatic route and goes to the jurisdictional officer. From the date of the application until the matter is closed, the registration is treated as suspended.

## How does the automatic approval of REG-16 work?

Once the return checks are cleared, the outcome depends on the type of registration and on how much tax was charged to registered buyers.

| Type of registrant | Condition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Composition, input service distributor, non-resident taxable person, OIDAR | Returns filed on time | Cancelled by electronic approval |
| Regular, casual or SEZ, with B2B output tax up to ₹2.5 lakh in every month since registration | Returns filed on time | Cancelled by electronic approval |
| Regular, casual or SEZ, with B2B output tax above ₹2.5 lakh in any month | GSTR-10 filed within 3 days | Cancelled by electronic approval |
| Same, but GSTR-10 not filed within 3 days | | Sent to the officer |

The ₹2.5 lakh figure is **not a turnover limit**. It is the output tax (CGST, SGST or UTGST, IGST and compensation cess) declared in GSTR-1, GSTR-1A or IFF on supplies to registered persons. The rule looks at every month from the date of registration up to the application, so a single large B2B month several years ago is enough to bring in the GSTR-10 condition.

The three days run from the due date of the returns identified at the "Pending for Returns Filing" stage or, where no return was pending, from the date the application was submitted. The application then shows as "Pending for Final Return" on the portal.

## What happens if the application goes to the officer?

The officer route under Rule 20 now runs on fixed timelines:

| Step | Time limit |
|---|---|
| Officer cancels (REG-19) or issues a deficiency notice (REG-03) | 15 days from receipt of the application |
| Applicant replies in REG-04 | 15 days from the notice |
| Officer approves or rejects after the reply | 15 days from the reply |
| No reply filed | Application deemed rejected (REG-05A) |
| Officer does not act within these limits | Application deemed approved (REG-19) |

Where the officer cancels the registration, GSTR-10 is still due within three months of the date of cancellation or the date of the cancellation order, whichever is later, under section 45. Separately, the officer may issue a notice in REG-34 proposing an earlier effective date; the reply is due in REG-35 within 30 days, and the effective date can be changed only once.

## What do you pay on stock when the registration is cancelled?

Section 29(5) requires a payment equal to the input tax credit on inputs held in stock, inputs contained in semi-finished or finished goods, and capital goods or plant and machinery, on the day before the cancellation date, or the output tax payable on those goods, whichever is higher. This is reported in REG-16 and again in GSTR-10, and can be paid from the electronic credit or cash ledger. Cancellation does not wipe out liability for earlier periods: tax, interest or penalty for periods before cancellation can still be determined later.

## Common mistakes when cancelling GST registration

From our work on closures and reorganisations, these are the patterns that hold up a cancellation most often:

- **Dormant months left unfiled.** Businesses that stopped trading months earlier often have nil GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B returns pending. The portal will not accept REG-16 until each one is filed, and returns older than three years from their due date can no longer be filed at all.
- **Missing the three-day GSTR-10 window.** Many applicants do not realise that one high-value B2B month in the past brings in the final-return condition. If the stock statement is not ready on the day of application, the case moves to the officer.
- **Stock figures that do not match the books.** Officers usually ask for the closing stock statement, the last balance sheet and the working of ITC reversal. Differences between the REG-16 stock value and the books are a common query.
- **Choosing an effective date before the last invoice.** A requested date earlier than the last tax invoice or e-way bill produces a mismatch and a notice.
- **Closing the bank account first.** The final payment of reversal or interest then has nowhere to come from.

## What comes next on 1 April 2027?

The same notification brings in a separate set of rules from **1 April 2027**: automatic suspension for non-filing of returns with a 30-day window to comply, automatic cancellation if the returns are still not filed, and system-based revocation in REG-21 within 180 days once the pending returns are filed. The Council has also recommended a second phase in which GSTR-10 details are furnished within REG-16 itself. Anyone planning to close a GSTIN should prepare the stock statement and ITC reversal working before filing REG-16, and keep the date on which the last monthly or quarterly return falls due in view, because the three-day window starts from it.
