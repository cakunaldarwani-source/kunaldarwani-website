From **1 November 2026**, a seller who sells goods through an e-commerce operator can take GST registration in another State without having its own office or godown there. Under the new **Rule 14B** of the CGST Rules, 2017, the seller declares the operator's warehouse in that State as its principal place of business, provided it is already registered in a home State, stays within an output tax limit of ₹2.5 lakh a month and the operator consents.

Rule 14B was inserted by the Central Goods and Services Tax (Amendment) Rules, 2026, notified through Notification No. 05/2026-Central Tax dated 26 October 2026. It gives effect to a recommendation of the GST Council at its 57th meeting on 8 October 2026.

## Why do online sellers need registration in other States?

Section 24(ix) of the CGST Act requires every person supplying goods through an e-commerce operator that collects tax at source (TCS) under section 52 to register, whatever its turnover. Marketplaces store stock in fulfilment centres across the country, so a seller whose goods sit in a warehouse in another State needs a GSTIN there.

Until now, registration in that State required a principal place of business in the seller's own name, backed by rent or ownership documents. Sellers without premises in the State often could not register there. Rule 14B removes that hurdle for goods sellers who meet its conditions.

## Who can register under Rule 14B?

All of the following conditions must be met:

| Condition | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Home-State registration | Already registered under Rule 9 in a State or UT where the seller is physically present |
| Same PAN | The new registration is taken under the same PAN |
| No physical presence | The seller has no physical presence in the new State or UT |
| Nature of supply | Intends to supply **goods** through an e-commerce operator required to collect TCS under section 52 |
| Output tax limit | Total output tax (CGST, SGST/UTGST and IGST) on supplies, other than to its own same-PAN registrations, does not exceed **₹2.5 lakh in any month** |
| Operator consent | The e-commerce operator consents to the declaration of its warehouse |
| Aadhaar | The applicant must have opted for Aadhaar authentication, unless it is a person notified under section 25(6D) |

Only one registration per State is allowed under this route: a person registered under Rule 14B in a State cannot obtain another registration under the same PAN in that State.

## How does the Rule 14B registration process work?

1. **Apply in FORM GST REG-01**, quoting the GSTIN of the home State and giving details of the operator's warehouse as the principal place of business. Other warehouses of the same or another operator in that State can be added as additional places of business.
2. **Operator consent:** the application is sent to the e-commerce operator, which must consent within **seven working days**. Without consent, the application is not processed further.
3. **Aadhaar and documents:** after consent, the home-State officer completes biometric Aadhaar authentication and document verification, unless these were already done.
4. **Physical verification** of the principal place of business in the home State follows. If no business is found there, the application is deemed rejected and this is communicated in FORM GST REG-05A.
5. **Grant:** registration is issued electronically. If physical verification is not completed within **30 days** of generation of the ARN, the application is deemed approved. Where the home-State registration was already granted after authentication and verification, the new registration is issued electronically once the operator consents.

The registration certificate shows the operator's warehouses declared in that State and the principal place of business address in the home State.

## What compliance follows after registration?

- **Books of account:** kept at the principal place of business in the home State for each Rule 14B registration, and produced when a proper officer asks.
- **Name board:** the seller need not display its name or GSTIN on the operator's warehouse.
- **Changes in warehouse address:** applied for in FORM GST REG-14, again with operator consent. Other changes follow the normal amendment procedure under Rule 19.
- **Operator's role:** the operator must appoint an authorised representative in each State or UT to arrange information required from these sellers.
- **Sellers already under Rule 14A:** a seller who opted for the simplified registration under Rule 14A in its home State must first withdraw from that option in FORM GST REG-32 before applying under Rule 14B.

## When can a Rule 14B registration be cancelled automatically?

The common portal cancels the registration electronically and informs the seller in FORM GST REG-38 if:

- the operator withdraws permission for the warehouse declared as principal place of business, and the seller does not apply within **30 days** to update the place details, or the operator's registration is cancelled;
- the seller's home-State registration is cancelled; or
- the operator changes the warehouse address and the seller does not file the amendment within 30 days.

If permission for an additional-place warehouse is withdrawn and no amendment is sought within 30 days, that place is simply removed from the certificate.

A seller who later sets up its own premises in the State and wants to leave this route can withdraw the option in FORM GST REG-32, giving principal place of business details and proof of physical presence.

## What should sellers check before applying?

- **Monthly output tax:** track tax on outward supplies every month. A seller whose output tax regularly exceeds ₹2.5 lakh in a month does not meet the condition and should consider normal registration with its own premises.
- **Operator agreement:** confirm that the marketplace will consent and keep the warehouse active, since withdrawal of consent can lead to cancellation.
- **Home-State compliance:** a cancelled or non-compliant home registration affects every Rule 14B registration linked to it.
- **Returns:** each Rule 14B GSTIN is a separate registration with its own returns, TCS credit and reconciliation with the operator's statements.

For States where the seller has, or plans, its own place of business, the [GST Multistate Registration](gst-multistate-registration-master-trn.html) facility is the related route.
