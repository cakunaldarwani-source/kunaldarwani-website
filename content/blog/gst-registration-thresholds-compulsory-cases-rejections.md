GST registration looks like a form-filling exercise. In practice a large share of applications receive a clarification notice, and many are rejected for avoidable reasons. Here is what to know before you apply.

## Turnover thresholds

Registration is required when **aggregate turnover** in a financial year exceeds:

| Supplier of | Most states (incl. Uttar Pradesh) | Certain states* |
|---|---|---|
| Goods only | ₹40 lakh | ₹10 lakh – ₹20 lakh |
| Services, or goods and services | ₹20 lakh | ₹10 lakh – ₹20 lakh |

*Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura apply a ₹10 lakh threshold to all suppliers. Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Puducherry, Sikkim, Telangana and Uttarakhand apply ₹20 lakh to suppliers of goods. Confirm the current position for your state before relying on the higher limit.

Aggregate turnover is computed on an **all-India PAN basis**. It includes taxable, exempt and export supplies and inter-state transfers to your own branches, and excludes GST itself and inward supplies taxed under reverse charge.

## Compulsory registration, whatever the turnover

Section 24 of the CGST Act lists cases where the threshold does not apply. The common ones are:

- **Inter-state taxable supply of goods.** Inter-state supply of *services* below the threshold is exempted from registration by notification.
- **Persons liable to pay tax under reverse charge.**
- **Casual taxable persons** and **non-resident taxable persons.**
- **Persons supplying through an e-commerce operator** that collects tax at source. Service providers below the threshold are exempt, and since October 2023 small suppliers of goods making only intra-state supplies through e-commerce operators can also operate without registration, subject to conditions.
- **Persons required to deduct TDS or collect TCS under GST.**
- **Input service distributors.**

## Regular or composition?

The **composition scheme** suits small businesses that sell mainly to consumers. Turnover must be up to ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh in special category states), or up to ₹50 lakh for eligible service providers. Tax is paid at a low flat rate on turnover, with quarterly payment through CMP-08 and an annual GSTR-4.

There is a catch. A composition dealer cannot charge GST on invoices, cannot claim input tax credit and cannot make inter-state outward supplies. If your customers are registered businesses, composition makes you less attractive as a supplier.

## Why applications get a REG-03 query or are rejected

- **Address proof mismatch.** The electricity bill or property tax receipt is in a different name from the rent agreement or consent letter, or shows a different address format.
- **Unsigned or unstamped rent agreements**, or a consent letter without the owner's ID.
- **Photographs or signboard missing** where the officer asks for a geo-tagged photograph of the premises.
- **Business activity not clear**, or HSN/SAC codes inconsistent with the description.
- **Authorised signatory** not authenticated on Aadhaar, or not properly authorised by a board resolution or partners' letter.
- **Residential premises** used without explaining how the business is actually run from there.

A reply to REG-03 must be filed within **seven working days**. Otherwise the application is rejected and you must start again.

## After registration

- Display the GSTIN and the registration certificate at the place of business.
- File returns from the effective date of registration, even nil returns. Since July 2025, returns cannot be filed more than three years after their due date.
- If your aggregate turnover crosses ₹5 crore in any year from 2017-18 onwards, **e-invoicing** applies.
- Generate **e-way bills** for movement of goods above ₹50,000.
