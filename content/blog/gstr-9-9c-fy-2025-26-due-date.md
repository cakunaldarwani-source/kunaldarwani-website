The annual return in Form GSTR-9 for FY 2025-26 is due on **31 December 2026**. Filing is optional for taxpayers whose aggregate turnover for the year is up to ₹2 crore, and the reconciliation statement in Form GSTR-9C must also be filed where aggregate turnover exceeds ₹5 crore. GSTN enabled both forms on the GST portal from 8 October 2026.

## What has changed for the FY 2025-26 annual return?

In its advisory of 13 October 2026, GSTN announced that GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C for FY 2025-26 are available on the portal from 8 October 2026. The tile appears only after **all GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B returns** for April 2025 to March 2026 have been filed. If even one month is pending, the annual return cannot be started.

FY 2025-26 is also the year in which the new GST rate structure (5%, 18% and 40%) applied from 22 September 2025. Many businesses therefore have the same item taxed at two rates within one year, and the HSN summary and tax-wise tables in GSTR-9 need to reflect both periods correctly.

## Who has to file GSTR-9 for FY 2025-26?

Section 44 of the CGST Act requires every registered person to file an annual return, other than the categories excluded by the law. In practice:

| Taxpayer | GSTR-9 for FY 2025-26 |
|---|---|
| Normal taxpayer, aggregate turnover above ₹2 crore | Mandatory |
| Normal taxpayer, aggregate turnover up to ₹2 crore | Optional (exempt) |
| SEZ unit or SEZ developer | Same as normal taxpayer |
| Registration cancelled during the year | Covered for the period it was active |
| Composition taxpayer | Not GSTR-9; separate annual return |
| Input Service Distributor, casual or non-resident taxable person, OIDAR supplier | Not required |
| TDS deductor (section 51) or e-commerce operator collecting TCS (section 52) | Not required |

The ₹2 crore exemption comes from Notification No. 15/2025-Central Tax dated 17 September 2025, which applies to the annual return for FY 2024-25 and later years. Aggregate turnover is computed on an all-India PAN basis, so a business with several GST registrations must add the turnover of all of them before deciding whether it is under the limit.

## When is GSTR-9C required?

Rule 80(3) of the CGST Rules requires a self-certified reconciliation statement in Form GSTR-9C where aggregate turnover for the year **exceeds ₹5 crore**. It is filed along with GSTR-9 by the same due date. GSTR-9C reconciles turnover, tax paid and input tax credit (ITC) reported in the returns with the audited financial statements, so the books for FY 2025-26 should be finalised before it is prepared.

| Aggregate turnover in FY 2025-26 | GSTR-9 | GSTR-9C |
|---|---|---|
| Up to ₹2 crore | Optional | Not required |
| Above ₹2 crore up to ₹5 crore | Mandatory | Not required |
| Above ₹5 crore | Mandatory | Mandatory (self-certified) |

## What is the late fee for filing GSTR-9 late?

Late fee is levied under section 47(2) of the CGST Act. Notification No. 07/2023-Central Tax dated 31 March 2023 reduced the rates for smaller taxpayers from FY 2022-23 onwards. The CGST late fee is:

| Aggregate turnover | CGST late fee per day | Maximum (CGST) |
|---|---|---|
| Up to ₹5 crore | ₹25 | 0.02% of turnover in the State/UT |
| Above ₹5 crore up to ₹20 crore | ₹50 | 0.02% of turnover in the State/UT |
| Above ₹20 crore | ₹100 | 0.25% of turnover in the State/UT |

An equal amount is levied under the State or Union territory GST law, so the combined late fee is double the CGST figure (for example, ₹50 per day for a taxpayer with turnover up to ₹5 crore).

Circular No. 246/03/2025-GST dated 30 January 2025 clarifies that where GSTR-9C is required, the annual return is complete only when **both** forms are filed. Late fee therefore runs from 31 December until the date on which the later of GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C is filed. The portal calculates it automatically.

## Why should GSTR-9 not be filed before reviewing ITC?

Section 16(4) of the CGST Act allows ITC on an invoice of FY 2025-26 to be claimed only up to **30 November 2026 or the date of filing the annual return, whichever is earlier**. If GSTR-9 is filed in, say, early November, any credit not yet claimed in GSTR-3B is lost from that date. Before filing, match purchase records with GSTR-2B and the Invoice Management System and claim any missed eligible credit by 30 November 2026.

## How long can an old GSTR-9 still be filed?

From 1 October 2023, section 44(2) bars filing of an annual return after **three years** from its due date. For example, a return that was due on 31 December 2023 (the normal due date for FY 2022-23) cannot be filed after 31 December 2026 unless the Government allows it by notification. Taxpayers with pending annual returns of earlier years should complete them before the window closes. The [three-year bar on GST returns](gst-returns-2026-ims-locked-gstr3b-three-year-bar.html) applies to monthly returns as well.

## What should be checked before filing GSTR-9?

1. **All GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B for FY 2025-26 filed**, otherwise the tile stays disabled.
2. **Outward supplies:** compare GSTR-1 (including GSTR-1A amendments) with the sales register and books, rate-wise for the periods before and after 22 September 2025.
3. **ITC:** reconcile GSTR-3B claims with GSTR-2B (auto-populated in Table 8A), reversals under rules 37, 37A, 42 and 43, and blocked credits.
4. **Credit claimed in the next year:** ITC of FY 2025-26 claimed between April and November 2026 is reported separately and not as current-year credit.
5. **Short payment:** any tax found unpaid can be declared in GSTR-9 and paid through Form DRC-03 with interest.
6. **HSN summary:** prepare HSN-wise outward and inward details as required for your turnover.
7. **Audited accounts:** where GSTR-9C applies, reconcile with the audited financial statements and explain differences.

Because GSTR-9 cannot be revised, careful reconciliation before submission matters more than early filing. Our page on [GST returns](../services/gst-returns.html) sets out the return cycle in more detail.
