The Import Export Code (IEC) is the basic registration for anyone importing into or exporting from India. Customs will not clear a shipping bill or bill of entry without it, and banks need it to process foreign trade remittances.

## Who needs an IEC

- Any person or entity **importing or exporting goods** for business
- **Service exporters** who want to claim benefits under the Foreign Trade Policy

Exemptions exist for imports and exports for personal use, for certain government departments and notified charitable organisations, and for specified low-value or courier consignments. Most businesses will need one.

## How to apply

The application is made online on the DGFT portal (dgft.gov.in) with:

- PAN of the business (the IEC number is linked to it)
- address proof of the business premises
- a cancelled cheque or bank certificate
- authentication by digital signature or Aadhaar-based e-sign

The government fee is ₹500. The IEC is normally generated electronically soon after a complete application is submitted.

## Applying on the DGFT portal, step by step

1. **Register** on dgft.gov.in with a mobile number and email address, verified by OTP. The email address becomes the username.
2. **Start the IEC application** and enter the entity details. For companies, the details are fetched using the CIN. Details of directors or partners are mandatory, and foreign directors can be added without a PAN.
3. **Upload the documents** as PDFs of up to 5 MB each:
   - proof of establishment or registration (partnership deed, trust deed, certificate of incorporation and so on)
   - proof of address, such as a sale deed, rent agreement or utility bill, with a no-objection certificate from the owner where the premises are not in the firm's name
   - a cancelled cheque or bank certificate for the firm's account
4. **Sign** with a digital signature or Aadhaar-based e-sign. Once signed, the application cannot be edited; a draft must be deleted and recreated instead.
5. **Pay the fee** through Bharatkosh by net banking, card or UPI.

## The annual update

Since 2021, every IEC holder must **update or confirm IEC details every year between April and June**. This applies even if nothing has changed. If you miss it:

- the IEC is **deactivated** after the window closes
- a deactivated IEC cannot be used for customs clearance or foreign trade benefits
- it can be reactivated by completing the update, but shipments in the meantime face delays at the port

Put the April–June update in your compliance calendar each year.

Where the system flags risks on an IEC, the holder must address them promptly as well, failing which the IEC can be deactivated even outside the annual cycle.

## Changes that need approval

Most edits, such as a new branch or bank account, are made through the "Modify IEC" option. Changes to the firm's name, the nature of the concern or partner details go to the Regional Authority for approval and show as "In Process" until approved. Branches cannot be deleted, only marked inactive. Mark one bank account as primary, because only the primary account is shared with CBIC (customs).

## Related registrations for exporters

- **AD code registration** – the bank's authorised dealer code must be registered at each port of export through ICEGATE, so that export incentives and IGST refunds are credited to your account.
- **GST Letter of Undertaking (LUT)** – file it on the GST portal each financial year to export goods or services without paying IGST. Without an LUT, you pay IGST and then claim a refund.
- **RCMC** – a Registration-cum-Membership Certificate from the relevant Export Promotion Council is needed to claim certain FTP benefits.
- **e-BRC** – once export proceeds are realised, the bank uploads realisation details, which are used for benefits and as proof for GST refunds.

## Common errors

- PAN and IEC names that differ from the GST registration
- bank account in the IEC that differs from the one used for export receipts
- an address change not carried into the IEC

## What we commonly see in practice

- The annual update is missed because nobody owns it. It is a two-minute confirmation, but a deactivated IEC can hold a container at the port.
- The IEC was applied for with a bank account that the business later stopped using, and export proceeds or refunds land in the wrong account.
- Businesses surrender an old IEC and then find they cannot apply again with the same PAN. Modify the existing IEC instead.

## A simple annual calendar

| When | What |
|---|---|
| April to June | Update or confirm IEC details on the DGFT portal |
| Before the first export of each financial year | File the GST Letter of Undertaking |
| After each change of address, bank or partners | Modify the IEC, then update customs and bank records |
