An income tax notice is not necessarily a sign of wrongdoing. Many are automated outputs of data-matching. Still, every notice has a deadline, and the consequences of ignoring one can be serious. Here is how to approach it.

## Common types of notices

References below are to the 1961 Act, which governs years up to FY 2025-26. The corresponding provisions continue in the Income-tax Act, 2025.

| Notice | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Defective return** (s. 139(9)) | The return has an error or omission, such as a missing audit report or a mismatch in TDS claimed. It must be corrected within 15 days, or the return is treated as invalid. |
| **Intimation** (s. 143(1)) | The return has been processed. Check for adjustments, such as disallowed deductions or TDS mismatches, and for any demand raised. |
| **Proposed adjustment** (s. 143(1)(a)) | The CPC proposes an adjustment and asks for your response within 30 days before it makes the adjustment. |
| **Scrutiny** (s. 143(2)) | The return has been selected for detailed assessment. |
| **Enquiry before assessment** (s. 142(1)) | A request for documents or information, during or before an assessment. |
| **Reassessment** (ss. 148A and 148) | The department believes income has escaped assessment. |
| **Penalty** | Proposed penalty for under-reporting, misreporting or non-compliance. |

## Checks before you reply

1. **Is it genuine?** Verify the notice on the e-filing portal under *Authenticate notice/order issued by ITD*. Every communication must carry a **Document Identification Number (DIN)**. A communication without a valid DIN is treated as invalid under CBDT's instructions.
2. **Which year, and which Act?** Note the assessment year or tax year and the section.
3. **Is it within time?** Scrutiny notices under s. 143(2) must be served within three months from the end of the financial year in which the return was filed.
4. **Who issued it?** Under the faceless scheme, most notices come from the National Faceless Assessment Centre. Jurisdictional issues are a recurring subject of litigation.
5. **What is the deadline?** Note it immediately. Adjournment requests are possible but not guaranteed.

## Replying on the e-filing portal

1. Log in and go to **Dashboard > Pending Actions > e-Proceedings**. Open the proceeding and the notice inside it.
2. Read the questions and the response date. If more time is needed, use the adjournment option before the due date and give a reason.
3. Prepare the reply as a covering letter with numbered answers, and the annexures as separate PDFs.
4. Upload within the limits: each attachment up to **5 MB**, up to **10 attachments**, and **50 MB** in all per submission. Split large files into logical parts.
5. Submit. The response cannot be edited or withdrawn afterwards, so check it before you click.

If a Chartered Accountant or other person is to reply for you, add them as the authorised representative for that proceeding first. Only one representative can be attached to a proceeding at a time.

## Reassessment time limits

Following the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024, for notices issued on or after 1 September 2024:

- **Three years and three months** from the end of the relevant assessment year as the general limit
- **Up to five years and three months** where the escaped income, represented in the form of an asset or expenditure, is **₹50 lakh or more**

Before issuing a reassessment notice, the officer must issue a show cause notice under s. 148A and give the taxpayer an opportunity to respond. Use this stage well: it is often the best chance to stop the proceedings.

## How faceless proceedings work

- Notices and replies are exchanged on the e-filing portal under **e-Proceedings**.
- A personal hearing by video conference can be requested where the proposed order is adverse.
- Before the final order, you receive a **show cause notice with a draft assessment order**. Respond to it in detail. It is not a formality.

## If the order goes against you

- **Rectification** (s. 154) for mistakes apparent from the record.
- **Appeal** to the Commissioner (Appeals) within **30 days** of receiving the order, followed by the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal.
- **Stay of demand** – generally available on paying a percentage of the disputed demand while the appeal is pending, as per CBDT guidelines.

## Documents usually asked for

- computation of income, the return and the audit report, if any
- bank statements for the accounts in question, with explanations for large credits
- purchase and sale deeds, broker contract notes and capital gains workings
- confirmations from lenders or debtors, with their PAN and bank details
- evidence for deductions claimed, such as investment proofs and donation receipts

## What we commonly see in practice

- Many notices come from a mismatch between the return and the AIS: a fixed deposit, a property sale reported by the registrar, or a cash deposit. A reply that reconciles each AIS entry usually settles them.
- Taxpayers often miss notices because the registered email is an old one or belongs to a former accountant. Keep the contact details on the portal current.
- Replies that attach every document without explaining them slow the case down. Officers respond better to a short note that links each document to the question it answers.

Respond on time, answer exactly what is asked, and support every statement with evidence. These three habits resolve most notices without escalation.
