"We need ISO certification for the tender" is a common request, and it often comes with a deadline of a few weeks. A meaningful certification needs a working management system. Here is what is involved.

## Who certifies whom

- **ISO** (the International Organization for Standardization) writes the standards. It does **not** certify organisations.
- **Certification bodies** audit organisations and issue certificates.
- **Accreditation bodies** – in India, NABCB under the Quality Council of India – check that the certification bodies are competent. Internationally they are linked through the IAF.

A certificate from a body **not accredited** by an IAF member may be rejected by buyers and tender authorities. You can check a certificate's validity on the IAF CertSearch database or on the accreditation body's website.

## The common standards

| Standard | Covers | Typical users |
|---|---|---|
| ISO 9001:2015 | Quality management | Any organisation; most tenders |
| ISO 14001:2015 | Environmental management | Manufacturers, exporters |
| ISO 45001:2018 | Occupational health & safety (replaced OHSAS 18001) | Factories, construction, logistics |
| ISO 22000:2018 | Food safety | Food processors, packaging |
| ISO/IEC 27001:2022 | Information security | IT, BPO, fintech, accounting outsourcing |
| ISO 13485:2016 | Medical devices quality | Device manufacturers and suppliers |

ISO 9001 is being revised, and a new edition is expected. Organisations certified to the 2015 edition will get a transition period once the new version is published.

## What the standards have in common

Most management system standards follow the same **Annex SL structure**. You will need to:

1. define the **context**, meaning internal and external issues and interested parties, and the **scope** of the system
2. set a **policy** and measurable **objectives**
3. identify **risks and opportunities** and plan actions for them
4. control **documents and records**
5. operate the core processes in a controlled way
6. **monitor and measure** performance
7. conduct an **internal audit** and a **management review**
8. correct **non-conformities** and improve continually

## A realistic timeline

| Phase | Typical duration for a small organisation |
|---|---|
| Gap assessment and planning | 1–2 weeks |
| Documentation and roll-out | 3–6 weeks |
| Running the system and keeping records | 4–8 weeks |
| Internal audit and management review | 1–2 weeks |
| Stage 1 and Stage 2 certification audits | 2–4 weeks |

The certificate is valid for **three years**, with **annual surveillance audits**. Missing a surveillance audit can lead to suspension.

## Be wary of shortcuts

Offers of a "certificate in three days, no audit" usually come from unaccredited bodies. Such certificates can be rejected, and in tenders they can lead to disqualification. A lean, well-designed system that your staff actually follow is worth more than a thick binder of borrowed procedures.
