Two people starting a business together usually look at three choices: a partnership firm, an LLP or a private company. This article compares the first two, since the choice between them comes up most often for small and mid-sized businesses.

## Liability

In a **partnership firm**, every partner is personally liable, without limit, for the firm's debts and for the acts of other partners done in the course of business. Creditors can go after a partner's personal assets.

In an **LLP**, a partner's liability is limited to the contribution agreed in the LLP agreement. A partner is not liable for another partner's wrongful act. The exception is fraud: where the business has been carried on with intent to defraud, liability can become unlimited.

## Formation and continuity

| Point | Partnership firm | LLP |
|---|---|---|
| Governing law | Indian Partnership Act, 1932 | LLP Act, 2008 |
| Registration | Optional with the Registrar of Firms (strongly advisable) | Mandatory with MCA |
| Minimum partners | 2 | 2 designated partners, at least one resident in India |
| Separate legal entity | No | Yes – can own property and sue in its own name |
| Change of partners | May dissolve the firm unless the deed provides otherwise | Does not affect existence |

Section 69 of the Partnership Act stops an unregistered firm from filing a suit to enforce a contract against a third party. For any firm that deals with customers on credit, registration is a practical necessity.

## Annual compliance

A partnership firm has no annual filing with any registrar. Its compliance is mainly income tax, TDS and GST.

An LLP must file:

- **Form 11 (Annual Return)** within 60 days of the end of the financial year, that is, by 30 May
- **Form 8 (Statement of Account & Solvency)** within 30 days of the end of six months from the year end, that is, by 30 October
- Event-based forms for changes in partners, registered office or the LLP agreement

An LLP's accounts must be audited if its turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh or its contribution exceeds ₹25 lakh in a financial year. Late filing of LLP forms attracts an additional fee of ₹100 per day per form, which adds up quickly.

## Taxation

Both are taxed in the same way under the Income-tax Act. Profits are taxed at 30% plus applicable surcharge and cess. The partners' share of profit is exempt in their hands. Interest on capital and remuneration paid to working partners are deductible within the limits in the Act, provided the deed or agreement authorises them.

Since **1 April 2025**, a firm or LLP must **deduct TDS at 10%** on salary, remuneration, commission, bonus or interest paid or credited to a partner, where the total for the year exceeds ₹20,000. This provision continues under the consolidated TDS section of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Many firms that never had to deduct tax on partner payments now need a TAN and quarterly TDS statements.

An LLP is not subject to dividend distribution and is not covered by the deemed-dividend rules, which apply to closely held companies. This gives it more flexibility than a company in drawing funds.

## Which one to choose

- Choose a **registered partnership firm** if the business is small, the partners trust each other fully and you want minimum paperwork.
- Choose an **LLP** if the business has meaningful credit exposure, you expect partners to change, or you want a separate legal entity without the compliance load of a company.
- Look at a **private company** if you plan to raise equity from investors.

## What does each one need to get started?

| | Partnership firm | LLP |
|---|---|---|
| Core document | Partnership deed on stamp paper (stamp duty varies by state) | LLP agreement, filed with the Registrar after incorporation |
| Where to apply | Registrar of Firms of the state, for registration | MCA portal: name reservation, then the incorporation form |
| PAN and TAN | Applied for separately after the deed is signed | Issued along with incorporation |
| From the partners | PAN, address proof, photographs | PAN, address proof, digital signatures and DIN for designated partners |
| Office proof | Rent agreement or ownership proof, with a utility bill | Same, plus the owner's no-objection where premises are rented |

## What we commonly see in practice

- **Deeds that are silent on remuneration or interest**, or that do not fix how remuneration is worked out. The deduction is then disallowed in assessment, and a fresh deed applies only from its own date.
- **Unregistered firms** that learn about Section 69 only when a customer defaults and a recovery suit is needed.
- **Dormant LLPs** that stop filing Form 8 and Form 11. The daily additional fees keep accruing and make a later closure costly.
- **LLP agreements** not filed after incorporation, or not updated when a partner joins or retires.
- **Monthly drawings** treated as remuneration since April 2025 without TDS being deducted or a TAN obtained.

A firm can later convert into an LLP under Section 55 of the LLP Act. If the conditions are met, the conversion is tax-neutral. Our article on entity conversions covers this.
