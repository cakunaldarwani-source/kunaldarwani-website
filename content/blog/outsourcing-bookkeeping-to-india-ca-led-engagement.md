Accounting firms and growing businesses in the US, UK, UAE, Canada and Australia increasingly use India-based teams for their bookkeeping and month-end work. Done well, the arrangement gives you timely, reviewed books and frees your local team for advisory and client work. Done badly, it creates rework. This article explains how to set it up well.

## What can be outsourced and what should stay local

**Well suited to outsourcing**

- transaction coding and bookkeeping in QuickBooks Online, Xero, Zoho Books, Sage, NetSuite or MYOB
- bank, credit card, payment gateway (Stripe, PayPal) and marketplace (Amazon, Shopify) reconciliations
- accounts payable processing and vendor statement reconciliation
- accounts receivable, invoicing and collections tracking
- month-end accruals, prepayments, fixed asset registers and depreciation
- management accounts and KPI reporting
- year-end workpapers and lead schedules for your accountant
- clean-up of backlog books and software migrations

**Better kept with your local licensed professional**

- signing and filing tax returns, such as IRS returns or HMRC filings, VAT returns and BAS
- audits, reviews and other attestation work
- tax advice requiring local licensing, and representation before local tax authorities

Under this division, the Indian team does the accounting and preparation work. Your local professional reviews it and takes regulatory responsibility.

## Three engagement models

| Model | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Dedicated accountant** – named staff for a set number of hours each month | Accounting firms with steady client volume; businesses with daily transactions |
| **Monthly close package** – fixed scope and deadline each month | Small and mid-sized businesses with predictable volume |
| **Project** – fixed deliverable | Catch-up books, clean-ups, year-end crunch, migrations |

## Time zones are an advantage

India Standard Time is 9½–10½ hours ahead of US Eastern time, 4½–5½ hours ahead of the UK, and 1½ hours ahead of the UAE. Work sent at the end of a US or UK business day can be processed overnight and is ready the next morning. A daily overlap window of two or three hours is usually enough for questions and review calls.

## Data security: what to ask for

1. **Work inside your systems.** The team should work in your cloud accounting software and document tools, not on local spreadsheets or email attachments.
2. **Individual user access with MFA.** Never share a master login. Each team member gets their own user, so access can be removed and changes traced to a person.
3. **Least privilege.** Give bank feeds read-only access. Do not grant payment authority unless it is specifically agreed and controlled.
4. **A written NDA and data-handling terms** in the engagement letter, covering India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and your own obligations, such as GDPR or UK GDPR where relevant.
5. **Offboarding** – a defined process to revoke access on the day a person leaves the engagement.

## What a good monthly close looks like

- Agreed **cut-off and delivery dates**, for example management accounts by the fifth working day
- A **close checklist** for each client, signed off each month
- **Review by a Chartered Accountant** before reports are released
- A **query log** in place of scattered emails, so open items are visible
- A short **monthly call** to discuss the numbers, not just the process

## The Indian side: regulation of the service provider

For the Indian firm, accounting services to overseas clients are an **export of services**:

- Under GST, the export is **zero-rated** and is supplied under a Letter of Undertaking without payment of IGST.
- Under FEMA, fees must be received in foreign currency through banking channels within the time allowed. The bank issues an inward remittance certificate.
- A firm of Chartered Accountants is regulated by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India and follows its Code of Ethics, including its rules on confidentiality and independence.

## What to have ready before the first month

- a list of entities, bank and card accounts, and payment platforms in scope
- the chart of accounts, coding rules and any recurring journals
- prior-year closing balances and the latest reconciliations
- named contacts for approving bills and answering queries
- your reporting calendar, including any lender or investor deadlines

## What we commonly see in practice

The most frequent cause of delay is not volume but **missing context**: receipts without a description, intercompany transfers with no note, and owner expenses mixed with business spend. A short coding guide and a weekly ten-minute query call clear most of this. The second pattern is **backlog hidden in suspense accounts**: books that look current but carry months of unreconciled items. A clean-up project before the monthly service starts avoids carrying those errors forward. The third is **access granted late**, which compresses the first close into a few days.

## Getting started

Begin with a **discovery call** to agree scope and software access. Then run a **parallel month**, in which the outsourced team closes a month that you also close, so you can compare results before switching over fully. Most clients settle into a steady monthly rhythm within two or three months.
