Tax year 2026-27 (April 2026 to March 2027) is the first year under the **Income-tax Act, 2025**, and the first full year of the **Labour Codes**. Payroll teams need to account for both.

## The new tax regime is the default

The new regime is the default for salary TDS. An employee who wants the old regime, with deductions such as house rent allowance, the ₹1.5 lakh investment deduction (formerly Section 80C, now Section 123 of the 2025 Act) and home loan interest, must tell the employer. Without such an intimation, TDS is computed under the new regime.

Key features of the new regime as currently applicable:

- **Standard deduction of ₹75,000** for salaried employees
- **No tax on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh**, through the rebate available to resident individuals
- Slab rates rising from 5% to 30% above ₹24 lakh
- **Employer's contribution to NPS** deductible up to 14% of salary

For most employees earning up to about ₹13 lakh, the new regime results in lower or nil tax. Employees with large home loan interest and HRA claims may still benefit from the old regime. A side-by-side computation at the start of the year helps employees decide.

## Monthly TDS mechanics

- Estimate annual salary and tax, and deduct evenly over the remaining months.
- Consider **other income** or **TDS from other employers** if the employee declares it.
- Deposit TDS by the **7th of the following month** (by 30 April for March).
- File the **quarterly salary TDS statement**, and issue the **annual TDS certificate** to employees by the prescribed date after year end.

Under the 2025 Act, the TDS provisions are consolidated in **Section 393**, and form numbers have been updated. Use the current versions of forms and utilities. Do not rely on templates from earlier years.

## Labour Codes: the payroll changes

- **Wage definition** – basic pay plus DA (and retaining allowance) must be at least **50% of total remuneration** for computing PF, gratuity, bonus and overtime. If allowances exceed 50%, the excess is added to wages.
- **Gratuity for fixed-term employees** after one year of service, on a pro-rata basis.
- **Timely payment of wages** – the Code on Wages sets time limits for payment of wages and for settlement on separation (within two working days of resignation, termination or retrenchment).
- **Records and payslips** – electronic records and payslips are recognised.

## A monthly payroll calendar

| Due by | What |
|---|---|
| 7th of the following month | Deposit TDS deducted from salaries (30 April for March) |
| 15th of the following month | PF contributions with the ECR, and ESI contributions |
| End of each quarter, by the prescribed date | Quarterly salary TDS statement |
| After the year ends | Annual TDS certificate to every employee |

## What we commonly see in practice

- **Regime choices and investment declarations collected late**, so most of the year's TDS falls in the last quarter and take-home pay drops sharply in January to March.
- **Proofs not checked** before the final quarter, which later shows up as a demand in the employee's return.
- **Employees who changed jobs mid-year** and did not declare the earlier salary, leaving tax short-deducted in both places.
- **PAN errors and unlinked PANs** in the TDS statement, which block credit for the employee.
- **Salary structures changed for the 50% test** without revising appointment letters or the payroll master.

## Year-end and exit processes

- **Full and final settlement** – compute leave encashment, gratuity, notice pay and recoveries, and apply TDS correctly. Exemptions for gratuity and leave encashment have limits.
- **Perquisite statements** and the annual certificate must reflect perquisites such as company car, accommodation and ESOPs at their prescribed values.
- **Reconcile** salary as per the books with the TDS statements before filing the fourth-quarter statement. Mismatches lead to notices for employees and for the employer.

## A practical checklist for 2026-27

1. Collect regime choices from all employees in writing.
2. Update payroll software for the 2025 Act's forms and section references.
3. Run the 50% wage test and update salary structures.
4. Provide for gratuity for fixed-term staff.
5. Fix a monthly payroll calendar covering inputs, approval, payment and deposit of statutory dues.
