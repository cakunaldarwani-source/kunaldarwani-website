On 4 February 2026, DPIIT notified a revised framework for recognising start-ups (G.S.R. 108(E)). It doubled the turnover ceiling, created a separate category for deep tech ventures and opened recognition to co-operative societies.

## Who qualifies now

| Criterion | General start-up | Deep tech start-up |
|---|---|---|
| Age from incorporation | Up to 10 years | Up to 20 years |
| Turnover in any year | Below ₹200 crore | Below ₹300 crore |
| Eligible forms | Private limited company, LLP, registered partnership firm, co-operative society | Same |

The entity must be working on innovation, development or improvement of products, processes or services, or have a scalable business model with high potential for employment or wealth creation. An entity formed by splitting up or reconstructing an existing business does not qualify.

**Deep tech** start-ups must show that their solution rests on new scientific or engineering knowledge, with significant R&D spending, novel intellectual property and real technical uncertainty. The longer age and turnover limits recognise that such ventures take longer to commercialise.

## What recognition gives you

- **Income tax holiday** – a deduction of 100% of profits for any three consecutive years out of the first ten, subject to separate approval by the Inter-Ministerial Board. The benefit is available to eligible companies and LLPs incorporated before 1 April 2030.
- **No angel tax.** The provision that taxed share premium above fair value was abolished from FY 2024-25 for all companies. The earlier start-up exemption process is no longer needed for this purpose.
- **Carry-forward of losses** – an eligible start-up company can carry forward losses despite a change in shareholding beyond the usual limit, subject to conditions.
- **Self-certification** under six labour laws and three environmental laws for a specified period.
- **Rebates on fees** for patent and trademark applications, and fast-tracked patent examination.
- **Public procurement** – relaxation of prior turnover and experience norms in many government tenders.
- **Faster exit** under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code for eligible start-ups.

## The tax holiday is a separate step

Recognition does not give the tax holiday by itself. The start-up must apply to the Inter-Ministerial Board. The Board looks at whether the business is truly innovative, not merely whether it meets the age and turnover tests. It is worth applying when the start-up expects taxable profits within its first ten years. Choosing *which* three years to claim deserves planning, because early-year profits are often small.

## Practical points

- Keep the innovation statement specific. "An app for X" is weaker than a description of what the product does differently and why it is hard to replicate.
- Keep statutory compliance current. Annual ROC filings and income tax returns are checked when benefits are claimed.
- Recognition ends automatically when the age or turnover limit is crossed, so track both.
