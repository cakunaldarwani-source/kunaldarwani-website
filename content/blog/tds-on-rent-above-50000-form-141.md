If you are an individual or HUF paying rent of more than ₹50,000 for a month, you must deduct TDS at 2% from the rent and deposit it with the government. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, the deduction is made only once, from the rent for the last month of the tax year (or of the tenancy, if it ends earlier), and is paid through **Form 141** within 30 days from the end of that month. Form 141 replaces Form 26QC for tax year 2026-27 onwards.

## Who has to deduct TDS on rent under the new Act?

Section 393(1) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 has a single table for TDS on payments to residents. Rent falls under two entries:

| Entry in section 393(1) table | Who pays the rent | Rate on rent for land or building | Statement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sl. No. 2(i) | Individuals and HUFs not covered by 2(ii), including salaried people and pensioners | 2% | Form 141 (Schedule A), no TAN |
| Sl. No. 2(ii) | Any other person, including individuals and HUFs whose business turnover exceeds ₹1 crore or professional receipts exceed ₹50 lakh | 10% (2% for plant, machinery or equipment) | TAN-based quarterly statement |

Most families renting a house or flat fall in the first entry. Here, "rent" means payment for the use of land, a building or land appurtenant to a building. Hire of furniture or equipment is not counted here.

The landlord must be a resident. Rent paid to a non-resident landlord is dealt with under the separate provisions for payments to non-residents, which have their own rates and filing requirements.

## When is the ₹50,000 limit crossed?

The test is monthly: TDS applies if the rent for a month or part of a month exceeds ₹50,000 during the tax year. A tenant paying ₹50,000 exactly has no obligation. A tenant whose rent rises from ₹48,000 to ₹52,000 in October crosses the limit for that tax year, and the deduction then has to be planned for the last month's rent.

## When is the tax deducted, and on what amount?

The Income Tax Department's FAQs set out the timing:

- **Property let for the whole tax year:** deduct at the time of payment or credit of the rent for the last month of the tax year (March), whichever is earlier.
- **Property vacated during the year:** deduct at the time of payment or credit of the rent for the last month of the tenancy.

The 2% is applied to the rent for the year, but the whole amount comes out of that one month's payment. An example for tax year 2026-27:

| Situation | Monthly rent | Rent for the period | TDS | Deducted from | Form 141 due by | Form 132 due by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full year, April 2026 to March 2027 | ₹60,000 | ₹7,20,000 | ₹14,400 | March 2027 rent | 30 April 2027 | 15 May 2027 |
| Vacated after September 2026 | ₹60,000 | ₹3,60,000 | ₹7,200 | September 2026 rent | 30 October 2026 | 14 November 2026 |

Surcharge and health and education cess are not added to the 2% rate.

## What if the landlord does not give a PAN?

Tax is then deducted at the higher of the rate in the Act, the rate in force, or 20%. There is a cap for this category: the TDS cannot exceed the rent for the last month of the tax year or of the tenancy. In the full-year example above, 20% of ₹7,20,000 is ₹1,44,000, but the deduction is limited to ₹60,000, the March rent.

With an incorrect PAN, the credit does not reach the landlord's Form 26AS or Annual Tax Statement.

## How do you file Form 141 and issue Form 132?

Form 141 is a single challan-cum-statement that replaced four earlier forms (26QB, 26QC, 26QD and 26QE). Rent paid by an individual or HUF is reported in **Schedule A**. The steps:

1. Log in to the e-filing portal with your own PAN. A TAN is not needed.
2. Go to e-File, then e-Pay Tax, select Income Tax Act 2025, choose New Payment and then Form 141.
3. Fill in Schedule A with the landlord's PAN and the rent and deduction details, and pay the tax online along with the form.
4. Keep the acknowledgement (ARN) and the challan identification number (CIN) generated after payment.
5. Register on the TDS-TRACES portal as a taxpayer, download **Form No. 132** (which replaces Form 16C) and give it to the landlord within 15 days from the due date of Form 141.

One Form 141 is filed per month of deduction. No further quarterly statement is needed for this category. Rent for tax year 2025-26 and earlier remains under section 194-IB of the 1961 Act and Form 26QC, so a tenant who missed a deduction for March 2026 deals with it under the old provisions. You can read more about the other new TDS forms in our article on [TDS return Forms 138, 140 and 144](tds-return-forms-138-140-144-q2-due-date.html).

## What does a late or missed deduction cost?

| Default | Consequence under the Income-tax Act, 2025 |
|---|---|
| Tax not deducted on time | Interest at 1% for every month or part of a month, from the date it was deductible to the date of deduction (section 398(3)) |
| Tax deducted but paid late | Interest at 1.5% for every month or part of a month, from deduction to payment (section 398(3)) |
| Form 141 filed late | Fee of ₹200 per day of delay, not exceeding the tax deductible, payable before the statement is delivered (section 427) |

These amounts keep growing until the default is cleared and appear as demands against your PAN on TRACES.

## Common mistakes with TDS on rent

From the cases we see each year, the same errors recur:

- **Assuming the landlord handles it.** The obligation is on the tenant. Landlords often ask for Form 132 only when filing their own return, by which time the deposit date has long passed.
- **Using the wrong form.** Some tenants obtain a TAN and file a quarterly statement as if they were a business deductor. For individuals under entry 2(i), Form 141 with PAN is the route.
- **Rent paid to joint owners.** Where a flat is owned by a couple or by siblings, the deduction and the Form 141 details should follow each owner's share and PAN, as per the rent agreement.
- **Mid-year rent increases.** Tenants whose rent crosses ₹50,000 after renewal of the agreement often overlook that the limit is now exceeded for that year.
- **Deducting from the wrong month.** Spreading 2% over every month is common practice, but the law places the deduction at the last month's payment or credit. Monthly deductions without a matching Form 141 for each month lead to mismatches.
- **Form 132 never downloaded.** The deposit is made, but the certificate is not generated, so the landlord's credit is questioned.

When the department follows up, the communication usually lists the PAN of the deductee, the months concerned and the computed interest or fee. A copy of the rent agreement, bank statements showing rent paid and the Form 141 acknowledgement are the documents typically needed to respond. ## What to do before March 2027

If your rent exceeds ₹50,000 a month and your tenancy runs through the tax year, keep the landlord's PAN ready now and plan to deduct 2% of the year's rent from the March 2027 payment, then file Form 141 by 30 April 2027. If you are moving out before March, the deduction falls on the last month of the tenancy, and the 30-day clock starts from the end of that month. Salaried tenants claiming house rent allowance through their employer should also check that the landlord's PAN on Form 124 matches the PAN used in Form 141.
