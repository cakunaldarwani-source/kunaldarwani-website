Salaried employees receive income tax notices mainly because the return does not match the information the department already holds, or because a deduction or exemption claimed in the return cannot be supported. TDS by the employer covers salary only; interest, capital gains and claims made directly in the return are checked separately. Most of these notices can be resolved by checking the facts and replying, or correcting the return, within the time given.

## Which income tax notices do salaried employees usually get?

References are to the Income-tax Act, 1961, which governs returns up to FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27). From tax year 2026-27, the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies and the corresponding provisions continue under new section numbers.

| Notice or communication | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Defective return, s. 139(9) | Return is incomplete or inconsistent | Correct within 15 days, or the return can be treated as invalid |
| Proposed adjustment, s. 143(1)(a) | CPC proposes to change your income or claims while processing | Agree or disagree within 30 days |
| Intimation, s. 143(1) | Return processed; may show a demand or reduced refund | Check every line; seek rectification if wrong |
| e-Verification / s. 133(6) letter | Information in AIS is not reflected in the return | Respond on the Compliance Portal with feedback and evidence |
| Scrutiny, s. 143(2) | Return selected for detailed assessment | Reply to each query with documents |
| Show cause, s. 148A | Department believes income escaped assessment | Reply in detail before any reassessment notice is issued |

## What are the most common reasons?

**1. Income in AIS not shown in the return.** Interest on savings accounts and fixed deposits, dividends, sale of shares or mutual fund units, and rent are reported to the department by banks and other entities. They appear in your Annual Information Statement (AIS). Leaving them out is the single most common cause of mismatch letters.

**2. Deductions or exemptions that cannot be supported.** Claims made in the return but not declared to the employer, such as donations, health insurance, HRA or interest on a housing loan, are compared with Form 16 and other data. The department has run a verification drive against bogus claims since July 2025. In December 2025 the CBDT launched a "NUDGE" campaign, sending SMS and email advisories to taxpayers who claimed donations under sections 80G and 80GGC to suspicious entities.

**3. More than one employer in the year.** Each employer may allow the standard deduction and the lower slab rates without knowing about the other. The combined tax then turns out higher than the TDS, and the shortfall shows up as a demand.

**4. High-value transactions.** Purchase or sale of property of ₹30 lakh or more, cash deposits aggregating ₹10 lakh or more in savings accounts, and large credit card payments are reported through the Statement of Financial Transactions (SFT). If your declared income cannot explain them, you may receive a query.

**5. Late filing or non-filing.** If TDS or other information shows income but no return was filed, a notice can follow.

## What should you do first?

1. **Confirm the notice is genuine.** Log in to the e-filing portal and check Pending Actions, or use the "Authenticate notice/order issued by ITD" service. A genuine communication carries a DIN.
2. **Read the section, assessment year and due date.** The section tells you what is being asked. The due date is what matters most.
3. **Download your AIS, TIS and Form 26AS.** Compare them line by line with the return and Form 16.
4. **Decide whether the department is right.** If the information is correct and you missed it, accept it and correct the return. If it is wrong, for example a joint account shown fully under your PAN, give feedback on the AIS and disagree with reasons.

## Can you correct the return instead of disputing?

Often, yes. For AY 2026-27, a revised return can be filed up to 31 March 2027, unless the assessment is completed earlier, following the Finance Act, 2026; revisions in the later part of that window attract a fee under section 234-I. After that, an updated return (ITR-U) can be filed within the extended time allowed by the Finance Act, 2025, on payment of additional tax that rises with delay. An updated return cannot be used to reduce income or increase a refund. Details of the revised and belated return deadlines are in our article on [belated and revised ITR for AY 2026-27](belated-revised-itr-ay-2026-27-last-date.html).

## What are the consequences of ignoring a notice?

- A proposed adjustment is made automatically after 30 days.
- A demand raised in an intimation carries interest and can be adjusted against future refunds.
- Penalty for under-reported income can be 50% of the tax on it, rising to 200% where the under-reporting is a result of misreporting, such as a false deduction claim.

## How do you reply?

Responses are filed online. CPC communications and assessing officer notices go through e-Proceedings. AIS mismatch letters go through the e-Verification tab of the Compliance Portal. Keep the reply short and specific: state the facts, quote the figures, and attach evidence such as bank statements, rent receipts with landlord details, donation receipts and Form 16.
