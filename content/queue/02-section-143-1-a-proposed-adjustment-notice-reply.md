A notice under section 143(1)(a) is a communication from the Centralised Processing Centre (CPC) proposing a change to your return before it is processed. It gives you 30 days to agree or disagree with each proposed change. If you do not respond in time, the return is processed with the adjustment, which often turns a refund into a demand.

## What is a section 143(1)(a) communication?

When a return is processed, the CPC checks it against the information in the return itself, the tax audit report and the department's records. Where it finds an item that falls within the permitted adjustments, the law requires it to tell you first and consider your reply. This intimation is shown on the portal as a "Prima Facie Adjustment u/s 143(1)(a)" or a "communication of proposed adjustment".

Returns up to AY 2026-27 are processed under section 143(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. From tax year 2026-27, processing is governed by section 270 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, which carries forward the same rule of prior intimation and a 30-day response window.

## Which adjustments can be proposed?

The CPC cannot change anything it likes. The adjustments allowed at the processing stage are limited to:

| Ground | Typical example |
|---|---|
| Arithmetical error in the return | Totals or tax computation do not add up |
| Incorrect claim apparent from information in the return | Deduction claimed beyond the statutory limit, or a claim that conflicts with another figure in the same return |
| Loss claimed in a return filed after the due date | Business loss carried forward from a belated return |
| Expenditure disallowance shown in the tax audit report but not considered in the return | Disallowance reported in Form 3CD but not added back |
| Certain deductions claimed in a return filed after the due date | Profit-linked deductions that require a return within the due date |

For returns from AY 2018-19 onwards, the law does not permit an adjustment at this stage merely because income appears in Form 26AS, Form 16A or Form 16 and is missing from the return. That kind of mismatch is generally pursued through verification letters or assessment.

## What triggers these notices most often?

- Deductions claimed in the return that differ from the employer's Form 16, without a clear reason in the return
- Claims that exceed limits, such as health insurance or interest deductions above the cap
- A tax regime choice that does not match the deductions claimed
- Losses carried forward from a belated return
- Disallowances shown by the tax auditor but not added back in the computation

## How do you respond on the e-filing portal?

1. Log in at incometax.gov.in and go to **Pending Actions > e-Proceedings**.
2. Open the proceeding named "Prima Facie Adjustment u/s 143(1)(a)" for the relevant assessment year and view the notice.
3. Click **Submit Response**. For each proposed adjustment, choose **Agree** or **Disagree**.
4. If you disagree, select the reason and add a short explanation. Attach supporting documents where relevant. A single attachment can be up to 5 MB, with up to 10 attachments and 50 MB in total.
5. Submit. The response cannot be edited or withdrawn afterwards.

Respond within 30 days of the date of issue, not the date you noticed it. Once the due date passes, the submit button is disabled.

## Should you agree or disagree?

**Agree** when the CPC is right. For example, you claimed a deduction you were not eligible for, or made a computation error. The return is then processed with the change.

**Disagree** when the claim is genuine and supported. Typical cases are a deduction proved by receipts but not given to the employer, or a figure the CPC has read from the wrong schedule. Give a precise reason, quote the amounts, and attach the evidence.

Where the return itself contains the mistake and the revision window is still open, filing a revised return can be cleaner than arguing the point. For AY 2026-27, the window runs to 31 March 2027, subject to the fee under section 234-I for later revisions.

## What happens after you respond?

The CPC considers the response and then issues the final intimation under section 143(1). It shows the income as processed and any demand or refund. The intimation must be sent within nine months from the end of the financial year in which the return was filed.

If the final intimation is still wrong:

- **Rectification** under section 154 can be requested online for mistakes apparent from the record.
- **Appeal** lies to the Commissioner (Appeals) within 30 days of the intimation, where the dispute needs examination of facts or law.
