Buying or selling property of ₹30 lakh or more is reported to the Income Tax Department by the sub-registrar, so the transaction appears in the Annual Information Statement (AIS) of both buyer and seller. A notice follows when the return does not explain the deal. For a buyer, that usually means the source of funds, TDS or the stamp duty value. For a seller, it usually means capital gains. Most such notices are answered with the sale deed, bank records and a clear computation.

References below are to the Income-tax Act, 1961, which governs transactions up to FY 2025-26. From tax year 2026-27, the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies and the corresponding provisions continue under new section numbers.

## How does the department know about your property deal?

Under the Statement of Financial Transactions (SFT) rules, the Inspector-General, Registrar or Sub-Registrar must report every purchase or sale of immovable property where the consideration, or the value adopted by the stamp valuation authority, is ₹30 lakh or more. The entry shows up in the AIS under your PAN. The buyer's TDS return in Form 26QB is also visible to the department.

## Why do buyers get notices?

| Issue | What the department checks | Provision (1961 Act) |
|---|---|---|
| Source of funds | Whether the price paid matches declared income, savings, loans or gifts | Section 69 (unexplained investment) |
| Price below circle rate | Whether the stamp duty value exceeds the price paid by more than the higher of ₹50,000 and 10% of the price | Section 56(2)(x) |
| TDS on purchase | Whether 1% TDS was deducted and deposited on property of ₹50 lakh or more bought from a resident | Section 194-IA |
| Cash payment | Whether a cash payment of ₹20,000 or more was made as advance or consideration for the property | Section 269SS (penalty on the person receiving cash) |
| Return not filed | High-value purchase by a person who did not file a return | Non-filing verification |

The most serious risk is an investment that cannot be explained. Income added as unexplained investment is taxed at 60%, plus a 25% surcharge and cess, under section 115BBE, with no deductions allowed against it.

## Why do sellers get notices?

- **Capital gains not reported.** The sale appears in the AIS, but the return shows no capital gains.
- **Sale price below stamp duty value.** Under section 50C, the stamp duty value is treated as the sale consideration where it exceeds the actual price by more than 10%.
- **Exemption claims.** Claims under sections 54, 54F or 54EC are checked for the conditions, such as purchase or construction of a new house within the time allowed, or deposit in the capital gains account scheme before the return due date.
- **Cash received.** Receiving ₹20,000 or more in cash as advance or consideration for the property attracts a penalty equal to the amount received, under section 271D.

## What if the AIS entry is wrong?

AIS entries are not always accurate. Common errors are the full value shown under each joint owner's PAN, a duplicate entry for the same deed, or a deal reported in the wrong year. Use the AIS feedback facility:

- **Information is not fully correct:** for joint ownership, enter your share.
- **Information relates to other PAN/year:** where the deal belongs to someone else or another year.
- **Information is duplicate/included in other information:** where the same deed appears twice.

Early feedback often prevents a verification letter.

## What documents answer a property notice?

1. Registered sale deed, with stamp duty valuation and ownership shares
2. Bank statements showing every payment, matched with the deed
3. Home loan sanction letter and disbursement statement
4. Evidence of other sources: sale of an earlier asset, gift deed from a relative with the donor's bank statement, maturity of investments
5. Form 26QB and the TDS certificate in Form 16B, for purchases of ₹50 lakh or more
6. For sellers: a capital gains computation with cost of acquisition and improvement, and proof of reinvestment for any exemption claimed

If the stamp duty value is disputed, the law allows a reference to a Valuation Officer. Raise this in the reply itself.

## How do you reply?

Verification letters about AIS information are answered on the Compliance Portal, under the e-Verification tab. Notices from an assessing officer, such as scrutiny or reassessment show cause notices, are answered under **Pending Actions > e-Proceedings** on the e-filing portal. Reply to each point separately, give a payment-wise reconciliation, and attach the documents in the order you refer to them. For general steps on reading a notice, see [how to read and respond to an income tax notice](income-tax-notice-how-to-read-and-respond.html).
