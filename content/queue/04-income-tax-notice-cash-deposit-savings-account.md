An income tax notice for cash deposits usually follows when your bank reports large deposits that your return does not explain. Banks report cash deposits aggregating ₹10 lakh or more in a financial year in savings and similar accounts, and ₹50 lakh or more in current accounts. The department then asks you to explain the source. If you can show where the cash came from, the matter usually closes. If you cannot, the amount can be taxed as unexplained money at an effective rate of about 78%.

References below are to the Income-tax Act, 1961, which governs years up to FY 2025-26. From tax year 2026-27, the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies and the corresponding provisions continue under new section numbers.

## Which cash transactions are reported to the department?

Under the Statement of Financial Transactions (SFT) rules, banks, co-operative banks and post offices report the following for each financial year:

| Transaction | Reporting threshold (aggregate in a financial year) |
|---|---|
| Cash deposits in savings and other accounts (not current accounts or time deposits) | ₹10 lakh or more |
| Cash deposits or withdrawals in current accounts | ₹50 lakh or more |
| Time deposits (other than renewals) | ₹10 lakh or more |
| Cash payment of credit card bills | ₹1 lakh or more |

These entries appear in your Annual Information Statement (AIS). The department compares them with your return and your income profile.

## What kind of notice will you receive?

- **e-Verification letter** on the Compliance Portal, often issued under section 133(6), asking you to confirm or explain the information in the AIS. This is the most common first step.
- **Scrutiny notice** under section 143(2), if the return is selected for assessment.
- **Show cause notice** under section 148A, if the department believes income has escaped assessment. It must be answered before any reassessment notice is issued.

Each has its own deadline. Treat the first letter seriously: a clear answer at the verification stage often prevents later proceedings.

## How is unexplained cash taxed?

If you cannot explain the source of money found in your accounts to the officer's satisfaction, it can be treated as your income under section 69A (unexplained money) or section 68 (unexplained credits). Such income is taxed under section 115BBE:

| Component | Rate |
|---|---|
| Tax | 60% of the amount |
| Surcharge | 25% of the tax |
| Health and education cess | 4% of tax and surcharge |
| **Effective rate** | **78%** |

No deduction, allowance or set-off of losses is allowed against this income. Interest and penalty can be added on top.

## What explanations are commonly given, and what proves them?

| Source of cash | Evidence that supports it |
|---|---|
| Business sales or receipts | Books of account, sales register, GST returns, cash book matching the deposits |
| Earlier withdrawals re-deposited | Bank statements and a dated cash flow statement linking withdrawals to deposits |
| Agricultural income | Land records, sale bills from mandi or traders, crop details |
| Gifts from relatives | Gift deed, relationship proof, the donor's bank statement or capacity to give |
| Sale of an asset | Sale agreement, buyer's details, receipt |
| Household savings | Reasonable amounts consistent with past income and returns |

Keep the explanation consistent with the returns already filed. An account that contradicts earlier returns weakens the case.

## Is depositing cash illegal?

No. Depositing your own money in your own account is lawful. The restrictions are on cash **receipts** between persons:

- **Section 269ST** bars receiving ₹2 lakh or more in cash from a person in a day, for a single transaction, or for transactions relating to one event. The penalty under section 271DA equals the amount received.
- **Section 269SS** bars accepting a loan, deposit or advance for immovable property of ₹20,000 or more in cash.

So the question is not the deposit itself but how the cash came to you.

## How do you respond on the Compliance Portal?

1. Log in to the e-filing portal and open the **Compliance Portal**, then the **e-Verification** tab.
2. Open the case for the relevant year and view each information item.
3. Choose the right response, such as "Information is correct", "Information is not fully correct" or "Information relates to other PAN/year".
4. Explain the source in the remarks and upload supporting documents.
5. Submit. You can add a further response later; the latest one is considered.

If the cash was income you forgot to report and the time limit allows, a revised or updated return may be the cleaner fix.
