A good reply to an income tax notice identifies the notice exactly, answers each query in the order asked, and backs every statement with an indexed document. It is submitted online on the e-filing portal, not by email or post, before the due date shown in the notice. Below is a structure that works for most notices, a sample outline, and the portal steps.

## What should every reply contain?

| Part | What to include |
|---|---|
| Identification | Name, PAN, assessment year (or tax year), section, notice date and Document Identification Number (DIN) |
| Reference | One line summarising what the notice asks for |
| Point-wise answers | Each query answered separately, using the notice's own numbering |
| Figures and reconciliation | Amounts quoted exactly, with a reconciliation table where figures differ |
| Legal position | Only where needed: the provision relied on, stated briefly |
| Annexure index | List of documents attached, numbered and referred to in the answers |
| Requests | Adjournment, hearing by video conference, or copy of material relied on, with reasons |
| Verification | Statement that the facts are true, with name and date |

## Sample reply structure

```
To: The Assessing Officer / National Faceless Assessment Centre
    (as shown in the notice)

Subject: Reply to notice under section ___ for AY ____
         DIN: ________  Notice dated: __/__/____

Name: ________________   PAN: __________

1. Reference
   The notice asks for details of ____________.

2. Reply to Query 1: [copy the query briefly]
   Facts: ____________________________________
   Figures: ___________ (reconciliation at Annexure 1)
   Evidence: Annexure 1, Annexure 2

3. Reply to Query 2: [copy the query briefly]
   ...

4. Submission
   In view of the above, the [income / claim / transaction]
   stands explained. [If an adverse view is proposed, a
   personal hearing through video conference is requested.]

Annexures
   1. ____________________
   2. ____________________

Verification
   The facts stated above are true to the best of my
   knowledge and belief.
   Name: ________  Date: __/__/____
```

Adapt the structure to the notice. A verification letter about one AIS entry may need only two or three short paragraphs. A scrutiny questionnaire may run to many queries.

## How should you prepare the annexures?

- **One document, one file where possible.** Name files by annexure number, for example `Annexure-01-Bank-statement-SBI.pdf`.
- **Highlight the relevant entries** in long bank statements, and add a covering summary.
- **Keep within limits.** On e-Proceedings, each attachment can be up to 5 MB, with up to 10 attachments and 50 MB in total for a submission. Compress or split larger files and use more than one submission if needed.
- **Use legible scans.** An unreadable document may lead the officer to ask for it again.

## How do you submit the reply on the portal?

**For CPC and assessing officer notices (e-Proceedings):**

1. Log in at incometax.gov.in and go to **Pending Actions > e-Proceedings**.
2. Select the proceeding and assessment year, and click **View Notices**.
3. Click **Submit Response**. Choose **Partial** if you will submit in instalments, or **Full** for a complete reply.
4. Enter your reply in the text box, or attach it as a PDF, and upload the annexures.
5. Submit and download the acknowledgement.

**For AIS mismatch letters (Compliance Portal):** open the **e-Verification** tab, select the case, give feedback on each information item, add remarks and upload documents.

**Things to remember:**

- A submitted response cannot be edited or withdrawn. You can file another submission with additional material.
- For CPC notices, the submit button is disabled after the response due date.
- An authorised representative can file the response on your behalf once authorised on the portal.

## What mistakes weaken a reply?

1. Replying after the due date without having sought an adjournment
2. Answering a different question from the one asked
3. Giving figures that do not match the return, the AIS or the documents attached
4. Attaching documents without explaining what each one proves
5. Making statements that contradict earlier returns or replies
6. Ignoring the show cause notice that accompanies a draft order. It is the last chance to respond before the order is passed.
