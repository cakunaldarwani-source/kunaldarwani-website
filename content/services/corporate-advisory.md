## Overview

Decisions on structure – which entity holds which business, who owns what, and how family members and investors participate – have long-term tax and legal consequences. Changing them later is expensive. The firm advises on these decisions with the tax, company law, FEMA and stamp duty angles considered together.

## When structuring advice is usually needed

- Before raising funds from investors or lenders, who will review the shareholding and group structure.
- When a family business passes to the next generation, or family members want to separate their interests.
- When one entity runs several unrelated businesses and the risks of one affect the others.
- When a non-resident relative or investor is to hold shares, which brings FEMA pricing and reporting into play.

## How the engagement works

1. We understand the business, the family or shareholder objectives and the constraints.
2. We map the current structure and identify the options, with their tax and compliance implications.
3. We recommend a structure and an implementation sequence.
4. We implement it, coordinating with legal counsel and valuers where required.

## Issues we commonly see in practice

Shares transferred at a price that does not match the fair value worked out under the tax rules, which can lead to tax in the hands of the buyer or the seller. Family arrangements agreed orally but never documented, so the tax and stamp duty position is unclear when assets later move. Properties held in an individual's name but used by the company without any agreement. Loans between group companies that were never approved or recorded properly under the Companies Act.

## What to keep ready

A chart of who holds what across the entities, the last three years' financial statements, and a clear note of what the family or shareholders want to achieve.
