## Overview

Businesses outgrow their original form. A proprietorship may need investors, a partnership may want limited liability, and a company may find the compliance load out of proportion to its size. The law allows conversion between forms. Done with care, a conversion is **tax-neutral**, and the business continues without interruption to its contracts, licences or history.

## When conversion is worth considering

- A proprietor wants to bring in an investor or co-founder, which needs a company structure with shares.
- Partners want limited liability without giving up the flexibility of a partnership, which points to an LLP.
- A small private company finds the board meetings, audit and filing load out of proportion and considers converting to an LLP.

## How the engagement works

1. We evaluate the target structure and the conditions for tax neutrality.
2. We prepare the conversion documents, valuations where needed, and the consents.
3. We file the conversion with MCA and the Registrar.
4. We transfer registrations, bank accounts, licences and GST credit, and update contracts.

## Issues we commonly see

Tax neutrality depends on conditions, such as the existing owners continuing in the new entity with a defined shareholding or profit share, and no consideration being paid other than shares or a share in capital and profit. A conversion that breaks a condition, even in a later year, can make the gains taxable. Other common gaps are property and vehicles still standing in the old name long after conversion, GST credit not carried over because the form for transfer of credit was never filed, and licences tied to the old PAN that need a fresh application rather than a simple change of name.
