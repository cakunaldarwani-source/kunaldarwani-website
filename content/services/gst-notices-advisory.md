## Overview

GST departments now use data analytics to compare returns with e-invoices, e-way bills, income tax data and suppliers' filings. Notices typically arise from mismatches in input tax credit, differences between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, or between GST turnover and income tax turnover, and from questions of classification and place of supply.

For periods from FY 2024-25 onwards, demands are raised under **Section 74A**, which sets a common time limit and a lower penalty where there is no fraud.

## Notices we commonly see

- **ASMT-10 scrutiny notices** pointing to differences between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, or between credit claimed and GSTR-2B.
- **Credit from suppliers whose registration was later cancelled**, where the department questions whether the supply took place.
- **Turnover differences** between GST returns and the income tax return or audited accounts, often caused by timing, advances or non-GST income.
- **E-way bill data** showing movements that do not match the returns.

## How the engagement works

1. We review the notice, the period and the legal provision, and check limitation and jurisdiction.
2. We reconcile the department's figures with your records and identify what is a genuine difference.
3. We draft a reply with legal grounds and evidence, and represent you at personal hearings.
4. We evaluate the order, and file an appeal or rectification where justified.

## What makes a reply work

Many demands we review arise from a reconciliation that was never done, rather than from tax that was actually evaded. A reply that explains each difference with a working, ties it to invoices and books, and sets out the legal position on the rest carries far more weight than a general denial. Time limits for replies and appeals are short and run from the date of the notice or order, so a notice should be reviewed as soon as it appears on the portal.
