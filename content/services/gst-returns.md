## Overview

GST compliance has become more automated and less forgiving. GSTR-3B liability is now auto-populated and locked from GSTR-1. Input tax credit is driven by the Invoice Management System. Returns cannot be filed once three years have passed from their due date. Monthly discipline in reconciling sales and purchases is what keeps credit flowing and demand notices away.

## Key return dates

| Return | Due date |
|---|---|
| GSTR-1 (monthly filers) | 11th of the following month |
| IFF (QRMP, first two months of a quarter, optional) | 13th of the following month |
| GSTR-3B (monthly filers) | 20th of the following month |
| PMT-06 tax payment (QRMP, first two months) | 25th of the following month |
| GSTR-3B (QRMP) | 22nd or 24th of the month after the quarter, depending on the state |
| GSTR-9 annual return | 31 December after the financial year |

The QRMP scheme is open to taxpayers with aggregate turnover up to ₹5 crore. The government can extend these dates by notification.

## How the engagement works

1. We collect sales and purchase data each month by an agreed date.
2. We reconcile sales with e-invoices and GSTR-1, and purchases with IMS and GSTR-2B.
3. We action IMS entries, file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, and report mismatches and vendor follow-ups to you.
4. At year end we prepare GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C, reconciled with the audited financial statements.

## Mismatches we commonly see

- Suppliers filing GSTR-1 late, so their invoices appear in a later month's GSTR-2B.
- Supplier credit notes appearing in IMS that need a decision, since accepting them reduces the credit available.
- Blocked credits, such as on certain motor vehicles or food and beverages, claimed by mistake.
