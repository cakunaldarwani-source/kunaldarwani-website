## Overview

An Import Export Code is a ten-digit code, linked to PAN, that is issued by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade. It is required to import or export goods, and to receive export benefits. Exporters of services also need it when they claim benefits under the Foreign Trade Policy. The IEC is issued for life, but it must be **updated every year** to stay active.

## Who needs an IEC

- Any business importing or exporting goods, since customs clearance requires it.
- Service exporters who want to claim benefits under the Foreign Trade Policy.
- Proprietors, firms, LLPs and companies alike: the IEC is issued against the PAN of the entity.

## How the engagement works

1. We file the IEC application with documents matching the PAN and bank records.
2. We register the AD code at the relevant ports and set up the GST Letter of Undertaking for exports without payment of IGST.
3. We file the annual update each year so that the IEC is not deactivated.

## Common issues we see

- The annual update missed, so the IEC is deactivated and customs clearance is held up until it is updated.
- Bank details on the IEC differing from those registered with customs, which can delay refunds and incentive credits.
- A change in address, partners or directors updated with GST but not on the DGFT profile.
- The IEC taken in a proprietor's personal capacity when the trade is meant to run through a company.

## What to keep ready

PAN, address proof and a bank certificate for the entity, a working mobile number and email for OTPs, and the DSC or Aadhaar of the person signing.
