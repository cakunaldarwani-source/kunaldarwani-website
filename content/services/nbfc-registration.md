## Overview

A company whose principal business is lending, investing in securities or similar financial activity must register with the Reserve Bank of India as an NBFC before it starts business. The RBI regulates NBFCs according to their size and risk, across Base, Middle, Upper and Top layers. Requirements on capital, governance and disclosure rise with each layer.

Small-ticket lending to low-income households can also be done through an **NBFC-MFI**, or on a not-for-profit basis through a **Section 8 microfinance company**, which does not require RBI registration within its limits.

## How the engagement works

1. We review the proposed business model and whether it triggers the principal business test.
2. We plan the Net Owned Fund and document its source, which RBI examines closely.
3. We prepare the business plan, projections and the policies RBI expects.
4. We file the application, respond to RBI queries, and set up post-registration returns and the statutory auditor's certification.
