## Overview

Employers must register with the **Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO)** once they employ 20 or more persons. They must register with the **Employees' State Insurance Corporation (ESIC)** once they employ 10 or more persons in areas where ESI is notified. Voluntary coverage is available below these numbers.

The four Labour Codes came into force on **21 November 2025**. Provident fund and ESI are now governed by the Code on Social Security, 2020. Its definition of "wages" changes the base on which contributions are calculated.

## How the engagement works

1. We check applicability for each establishment and register the employer.
2. We generate UANs and IP numbers for employees and link their KYC.
3. We prepare and file monthly ECRs and challans by the 15th of the following month.
4. We review salary structures against the 50% wage rule and advise on the cost impact.

## ESI and PF contribution basics

Under ESI, the employer contributes 3.25% and the employee 0.75% of wages. As per ESIC, employees earning up to ₹21,000 a month (₹25,000 for persons with disability) are covered. Contributions are payable within 15 days after the end of the month. PF contributions are payable by both the employer and the employee as a percentage of wages, and are deposited through the monthly ECR.

## Common issues we see in practice

- Registration delayed after the employee count crosses the threshold, which creates arrears along with interest and damages.
- Workers engaged through contractors left out without checking whether the contractor is registered and depositing contributions.
- UANs not linked to Aadhaar and bank accounts, so the ECR or employee claims are rejected.
- Different wage figures used for PF and ESI with no reconciliation.
- Employees who have left not marked as exited on the portals.

Keeping joining dates, monthly wage registers and contractor registration details in one place makes inspections and notices far easier to handle.
