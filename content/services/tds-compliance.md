## Overview

Tax deducted at source is the department's main tool for tracking transactions, and a frequent source of interest, late fees and disallowances. The Income-tax Act, 2025 consolidates the TDS provisions into a single section with a table of payments and rates. The department matches each deductee's claim against the deductor's statement, so errors on the deductor's side cause problems for the deductee too.

## How the engagement works

1. We review the types of payment and set up the correct rates and thresholds in your accounting system.
2. Each month we compute TDS/TCS, prepare challans and confirm deposit by the 7th.
3. Each quarter we file the statements and issue certificates.
4. We monitor TRACES for defaults and file corrections promptly.

## Quarterly TDS statement due dates

| Quarter | Due date |
|---|---|
| April to June | 31 July |
| July to September | 31 October |
| October to December | 31 January |
| January to March | 31 May |

From tax year 2026-27, the salary statement is filed in Form No. 138 (earlier Form 24Q) and the statement for payments to non-residents in Form No. 144 (earlier Form 27Q). The annual salary certificate is Form No. 130 (earlier Form 16), issued from TRACES by 15 June.

## What we commonly see in practice

- Wrong or invalid PAN of the deductee, which pushes the rate up and leaves the deductee without credit.
- TDS deducted under the wrong category or rate.
- Challan details entered wrongly in the statement, so the deposits do not match the deductions reported.
- Statements filed late, attracting a late fee for each day of delay.
- Lower deduction certificates applied for after payments have already started.

Keep vendor PANs validated, and reconcile the TDS ledger with challans every month instead of at the quarter end.
